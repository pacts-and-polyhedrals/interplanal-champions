## Introduction: The Infernal Scripture of Binding

Welcome, faithful servant of Asmodeus, to the *Infernal Codex of Contractual Magic*. This tome—copied painstakingly by the scribes of the Asmodeun Scriptorium—expands upon the sacred practices of infernal contract magic. Within these pages, wizards, clerics, and paladins sworn to the Lord of the Nine Hells will find the codified **codes of practice**, powerful **binding rituals**, and the dark **theology** underpinning soul-bound agreements. You will also discover a hierarchy of Hell's denizens (from the lowliest imp to the dread Archduke), guidelines for **summoning and negotiating** with infernal entities, three complete **subclasses**, and a full ladder of **contract spells from cantrip to ninth circle**. All is presented with the proper ritualistic phrasing and ecclesiastical commentary of Asmodeus's own clergy, that the reader may wield these powers both lawfully and effectively in service to the Dark Lord. *In the beginning was the Pact, and in the Pact was Power.* So it is written, and so you shall learn.

## Infernal Codes of Practice

Before delving into spells and rituals, one must understand the **Codes of Infernal Practice**—the principles by which all infernal contract magic is governed. The Church of Asmodeus holds these tenets as law, ensuring that every deal struck and every spell cast furthers Hell's order and Asmodeus's glory. Adhering to these codes isn't mere bureaucracy; it is an act of devotion.
:
* **Honor the Letter of the Law.** An infernal contract is sacred. Never break a signed contract, and never default on a promise sealed by magic. To do so is to invite immediate damnation or worse. As Asmodeus himself purportedly never breaks his word, so must his followers treat each contract as inviolable. The sanctity of law is what separates devilry from the chaos of demons, and in the Church this principle is absolute.
:
* **Specify Every Clause.** *The devil is in the details* is not just a saying—it is a warning and a strategy. Always be precise and exhaustive in wording any magical pact. Every condition, every reward, and every punishment for breach should be clearly outlined. Vague terms are open doors through which clever fiends will slither. A proper infernal contract traditionally includes five essential elements—**Consideration, the Offer, Obligation, Fine Print, and Acceptance**—and the wise practitioner will include additional clauses as needed to close any loopholes.
:
* **Leverage Consideration.** No soul or service comes free. In Hell's economy, **souls are currency** and **services are commodities**. When crafting a contract or casting a binding spell, ensure there is an exchange of value: a mortal's oath, a blood tithe, a service to be rendered. This consideration fuels the magic of the pact and gives both parties incentive to uphold their end. Even a minor deal might require a token sacrifice, while greater boons demand commensurate payment—often one's **mortal soul**.
:
* **Involve the Hierarchy.** As servants of Asmodeus, you are part of Hell's grand hierarchy. Significant pacts—especially those involving souls—should be witnessed or sanctioned by higher-ranking devils or Church superiors. This not only provides additional magical enforcement (for a superior devil will eagerly punish an oathbreaker) but also weaves the pact into Hell's bureaucracy. An infernal contract gains legitimacy when a contract devil, erinyes, or ordained priest of the Church cosigns it with Hell's authority.
:
* **Maintain Documentation.** The Asmodeun Scriptorium teaches that *"our contracts are our prayers; our ledgers, the holiest of texts."* Always record the details of any pact or spell effect in an official ledger or scroll. Many clergy carry **Infernal Record** books inscribed with devilish script. These records are more than memory aids—once written and blessed, they become magically linked to the contract itself. Scriptorium archivists reference them to check for any sign of circumvention or impending breach.
:
* **Punish Betrayal, Reward Fidelity.** The faithful know that rewarding those who honor their bargains—with promised boons, infernal blessings, or promotion within cult ranks—reinforces the value of contracts. Conversely, any who dare violate an infernal contract must be made an example of. Curses such as the **Mark of the Malefactor** (a brand placed by clergy upon contract-breakers) or the dispatching of a hellish bounty hunter are appropriate responses. This code ensures that word spreads in both the mortal realm and the Hells: treachery against Asmodeus's pact will be repaid a hundredfold.

**Authored by the Scriptorium of Asmodeun**

**For use in any infernal pact binding spellcasters to devils**

## Binding Rituals and Soul Contracts

Infernal contract magic centers on **binding rituals** and **soul-based agreements**: the carefully orchestrated ceremonies and spells that tie participants to their promises, with Hell as both witness and enforcer. In Asmodeus's church, a contract is not merely ink on parchment—it is a living construct of magic, law, and soul energy, quite literal in its power to compel.
:
**The Ritual of the Scarlet Quill.** The most common binding rite taught to infernal clerics involves the Scarlet Quill. In a solemn ceremony, the contract is written on prepared parchment of calfskin—or, more ominously, cured lemure flesh—and inked with a quill dipped in the contracting parties' blood. As the quill scratches out the terms, a hush falls; devils nearby feel the tingle of the pact forming. Once both parties sign, the document rolls itself and glows faintly with infernal sigils. At this moment each signer feels a **soul link** take hold: a dull tug at the core of their being, affirming that a portion of their soul is now held in escrow by Asmodeus's court. The ritual concludes with an incantation: *"By the Pact Primeval and the blood here bound, so is this covenant sealed. In the name of Asmodeus, let none break it."*
:
**Soul Vows and Oaths.** Not all contracts are written; some are spoken vows witnessed by magic. Infernal priests may perform **Binding Oath** ceremonies in which a supplicant swears aloud before an altar of Asmodeus. A *zone of truth* or similar effect ensures honest intent. When the vow is spoken, the presiding cleric traces the Sign of Hell's Pact over the one making the promise; this manifests as a faint infernal rune on the forehead or hand, invisible except to devils and the faithful. The oath is then as binding as any written contract. In rules terms this functions much like *geas* or the **Seal of the First Pact** spell in Part IX, invoked as a divine rite rather than a prepared casting. An oath might read: *"I swear to deliver the heretic alive to the Church by the next full moon, or my soul be forfeit."* If the deadline passes unfulfilled, the penalty clause activates.
:::
**Mechanics of Enforcement.** Contracts are enforced by **infernal clauses** embedded during their creation—conditional curses or boons. A clause might read: *"Should the mortal fail in their obligation, let their sight be forever taken by the Lord of Lies."* In practice this is a stored spell effect held quiescent on the target and triggered by breach. Clerics crafting such agreements use **Binding Clause** (Part IX), contingent *bestow curse*, or call upon a contract devil to weave the punishment into the fabric of the pact. Severe agreements may invoke an automatic **planar summons**: break faith, and an infernal creature is dispatched to hunt the transgressor. On the other side, a fulfilled contract can carry a **reward clause**—divine healing, a **Devil's Mark** granting fire resistance, or fluency in Infernal as recompense for loyal service.

**Soul Transference Agreements.** The most solemn of infernal contracts involve the outright exchange or collateral of a **soul**. Such a contract states that upon the mortal's death—or upon breach—their soul is claimed by Asmodeus. These tap directly into the cosmic law established by the Pact Primeval, the ancient agreement that first granted devils rights to mortal souls. Because of that primordial pact, only divine intervention can release a soul once a devil has lawfully claimed it. When such a contract is signed, a **soul tether** forms, connecting the mortal's life force to a hellish receptacle—sometimes an actual soul coin minted for the occasion, sometimes a phylactery-like vessel kept by the Church. If the soul is forfeit, the tether yanks it down into the Nine Hells upon death, bypassing the normal judgments of the afterlife.
:
In all cases, the **power of an infernal pact** lies in its **enforceability**. The combination of lawful wording, sacramental magic, and Hell's backing makes it nearly impossible for a bound party to escape their obligations without dire consequence. This is why mortals fear devils' bargains—and why Asmodeus's faithful so zealously master the art.

{{note
##### On Escaping a Contract
Three roads out exist, and the Scriptorium hates all of them.
:
**Performance.** Do the thing. Boring, effective, and by far the most common ending.
:
**Defect.** Find a clause the drafter left ambiguous and argue it before an infernal magistrate. This is the Loophole rule opposite—and the reason Article IV exists.
:
**Intervention.** A god, an archdevil with a grudge, or a *wish*. Expensive, and it leaves you owing someone new.

# The Ledger
## A Subsystem for Playable Contracts

{{wide
A devil's bargain that lives entirely in GM fiat is a trap; a devil's bargain with numbers attached is a *game*. The Ledger gives infernal contracts a shared, visible state that players can scheme against. Every rule below is optional, but they are designed to interlock.
}}

### Step One: Drafting

Drafting a contract requires an hour of work, writing implements, and a stated **Purpose**. The drafter makes an **Intelligence check** using calligrapher's supplies, or Intelligence (Investigation) if untrained in them.

| Contract Scope | Draft DC | Base Loophole Rating |
|:---|:---:|:---:|
| A single favour, one day or less | 10 | 3 |
| A season of service; a modest boon | 13 | 4 |
| A life's service; a named artifact | 16 | 5 |
| A soul, a bloodline, a kingdom | 19 | 6 |
| A cosmic clause; a god's attention | 22 | 8 |

**Loophole Rating** is the number of exploitable gaps in the finished document. A successful draft check reduces the rating by 1, and by 1 more for every 5 points by which the check exceeds the DC. A failed check *increases* it by 1. Spending an additional hour and 50 gp of legal reference material grants advantage.
:
The **Quill of Binding** spell reduces the Loophole Rating by 1 (minimum 1). A contract devil retained as co-counsel reduces it by 2 but claims a share of the consideration. **No contract can be reduced below Loophole Rating 1.** Hell insists. A perfect contract would end the game, and Hell is very fond of the game.

### Step Two: Finding the Gap

A party seeking to escape a contract must *find* its loopholes before exploiting them. Studying a contract for 10 minutes allows an Intelligence (Investigation) check against the **Scrutiny DC** (8 + twice the Loophole Rating). Each success reveals one loophole; each loophole revealed can be argued once.
:
Arguing a loophole is a social contest before whatever authority holds jurisdiction—an imp notary, a bone devil auditor, or in extremis the Court of Nessus itself. The arguing character makes a Charisma (Persuasion) check, or Intelligence (Religion) if arguing infernal precedent, against DC 10 + the contract's Draft DC ÷ 2. Success voids one Article. Failure costs the arguer 5% of their soul in **court fees**, because of course it does.

\page

### Step Three: The Soul Tithe Track

Soul-collateral contracts are tracked as a percentage from 0 to 100. Mark it openly on the table; dread is a mechanic.

| Tithe | Condition |
|:---:|:---|
| 5–25% | **Marked.** An infernal brand appears somewhere on the body. Fiends recognise it on sight and treat you as under contract. |
| 30–50% | **Encumbered.** Your maximum hit points are reduced by 5% per 10% tithed. Devils gain advantage on Insight checks against you. |
| 55–75% | **Indentured.** You have disadvantage on saving throws against effects created by fiends. Once per long rest the holder of your contract can compel you to take or forgo one action; a Charisma save (DC 8 + the holder's proficiency bonus + Charisma modifier) resists. |
| 80–95% | **Foreclosing.** You cannot regain hit points from spells cast by celestials or good-aligned clerics. You dream in Infernal, in legalese, and in the second person. |
| 100% | **Claimed.** The soul departs on the holder's terms. Whether the body survives as a husk, or the character rises as a devil under the GM's control, is set by the contract's Article VI. |

**Reducing the Track.** Tithe can be bought back only through performance of the contract, a *wish*, divine intervention, or by delivering an equivalent soul in substitution—the last being how most such contracts propagate. Souls delivered in substitution must be given freely by the substitute. Devils are scrupulous about this; a stolen soul is worthless to them, and a *coerced* one is worth about a third.

{{descriptive
##### GM Guidance: Ruling in Bad Faith Correctly
A devil never lies about the terms. It lies about what the terms will feel like. When a player asks a direct question about a clause, answer honestly and in detail—then let them discover, weeks later, that Article II said "safe passage" and said nothing whatsoever about *comfortable* passage, or about their horse.
:
The three infernal rulings, in order of preference: the letter over the spirit; the earlier contract over the later; the more specific clause over the more general. Announce these at the table early. A player who knows the rules of the trap can enjoy walking into it.

## Theology of Infernal Contracts
:
To fully grasp the significance of contractual magic, one must understand how infernal contracts fit into the **theology of Asmodeus's Church**. According to church doctrine—preserved in texts such as the *Catechism of the Ruby Lord* and annotated within the Scriptorium's volumes—the binding of souls through oaths is not mere spellcraft. It is a reification of Asmodeus's divine will and of the cosmic order he engineered.
:
**The Pact Primeval—The First Contract.** Church scholars speak reverently of the *Pact Primeval*, an accord struck between Asmodeus and the gods of law at the dawn of creation. In this legend, Asmodeus convinced the celestial powers to allow devils to punish the souls of the wicked. This created a fundamental cosmic law: devils could offer salvation or power to mortals in exchange for the right to damn their souls—a transaction meant to keep the multiverse's evil in check. The Pact is often called the **First Contract**, and it established Asmodeus not only as ruler of Hell but as the ultimate arbiter of infernal law. *Every infernal contract made by a cleric or paladin is a humble echo of that first great bargain.* Thus, when you engage in contractual magic, you enact a microcosm of Asmodeus's ancient deal. So long as your contracts are made in accordance with Hell's laws, **Asmodeus's favour** empowers them, making your bindings harder to break and your curses especially potent.
:
**Law as Divine Instrument.** Asmodeus is unique among evil gods for his devotion to law. The Church contrasts this with the chaos of demons. One scripture reads: *"Chaos is the tyranny of chance; Law is the tyranny of control."* Contracts, pacts, and strictures are therefore holy instruments. A cleric of Asmodeus might sermonize that when a contract is signed, **two fates are bound and order is increased in the world**, which is inherently virtuous from Hell's perspective. This twisted morality holds that **betrayal is the ultimate sin**, because it represents chaos breaking lawful bonds. Enforcing a contract magically becomes an almost sacramental act: you punish sin—the sin of oath-breaking—and uphold the virtue of obedience.
:
**The Value of Souls.** The Church teaches that souls are the purest form of currency in the multiverse. A soul freely given, or fairly won by contract, is more potent than one taken by force or deceit, because by Hell's law it belongs to Asmodeus indisputably. Each bargain strengthens Hell's armies and simultaneously deprives good and chaos of recruits. It is a spiritual economy: contracts bring in a steady tithe of souls much as faithful tithe coin at a mortal temple. High Priestesses have called contract-making *"the holiest form of conversion"*—converting a wayward mortal into a servant of Hell by signed parchment rather than pious preaching.
:
**Ecclesiastical Commentary.** Woven through the Scriptorium's copies are marginal notes from generations of infernal scholars. One recounts a Duke of Hell rewarding a cleric who brought in a dozen souls in a year with a personal audience and a hell-forged rod. Another warns: *"Beware the clever mortal lawyers; one need not be virtuous to wriggle out of a poorly forged pact. Sloth in drafting is an invitation to the Abyss."* The Scriptorium stresses that a contract is a prayer **in action**. Unlike a plea to a deity, a contract *compels* a result. Abusing contract magic for trivial or personal ends is cautioned against—not from ethical concern, but because it risks the ire of higher devils who sense wasted opportunity.

## Hierarchy of Hell and Infernal Agents

No study of infernal magic is complete without an understanding of **Hell's hierarchy**. The Nine Hells run on a strict caste system. Each devil has a rank, a purpose, and an overbearing superior to whom it answers. As a practitioner, you must know *who* you are dealing with when invoking contractual clauses.
:
* **Lemure.** The lowest form of devil: tormented blobs of quivering flesh, souls stripped of identity. They cannot speak and follow only simple orders. Lemures have no role in contracts except *as currency or as punishment*. Many lemures are the remnants of mortals who signed away their souls, and they serve as a reminder of the fate awaiting the damned.
:
* **Imp.** Small, cunning, wickedly clever; messengers, informants, and familiars. An imp might present a contract to a mortal or spy on a subject of a pact. They are the literal devil on the shoulder encouraging a signature. The Church often binds imps into quills or tomes as assistants that ensure all contractual language is Hell-approved.
:
* **Spined Devil.** Flying couriers and sentinels, used to deliver contracts across dangerous distances. If one lurks after a signing, it is waiting to snatch the document and whisk it to its master's archive.
:
* **Bearded Devil.** Fierce foot-soldiers, more known for combat than subtlety—but they make excellent muscle for *enforcement*. When a contract stipulates immediate seizure of a defaulter, a barbazu's clawed hands do the seizing.
:
* **Chain Devil.** Sadistic jailers who relish binding and torment; the personification of enforcement clauses. Many infernal contracts include the seal of a chain devil as a notary mark, symbolising the fate of oathbreakers.
:
* **Bone Devil.** Gaunt taskmasters and mid-tier bureaucrats. A bone devil might oversee **all soul contracts in a region**, keeping meticulous ledgers of quotas. The Church occasionally invites one to audit its own books—a terrifying rite known as the Infernal Audit, and the inspiration for the cleric feature of the same name.

* **Horned Devil.** Hulking winged enforcers who command lesser legions. If a mortal cheats a devil out of a promised soul, a cornugon may be dispatched as a bounty hunter. Lazy and arrogant by nature—but relentless once tasked by an archdevil.

They also serve as **witnesses** to high-profile pacts, standing guard through the signing and guarding the mortal's soul until collection.

* **Ice Devil.** Greater devils and master strategists. A momentous contract—a king trading his kingdom's fate for power—may attract a gelugon assigned to orchestrate the long game around it. Their presence means the contract's repercussions will be far-reaching and meticulously planned.
:
* **Pit Fiend.** Tyrants second only to the archdukes. Their involvement signals a soul of extreme value or stakes of enormous scale. Pit fiends hold the authority to **rewrite contracts**, voiding or amending the deals of lesser devils when it suits Hell's interests.
:
* **Contract Devil.** Rarely seen on a battlefield and never seen without a document. A contract devil's function is to draft, notarize, and arbitrate. It cannot be bribed with treasure and takes no interest in violence; it wants *cases*. Offer it an interesting legal question and it will work for very little. Offer it a boring one and it will bill you for centuries.
:
* **Archdevils and the Lords of the Nine.** Above all stand the unique archdevils, with Asmodeus supreme. Each has a philosophy on contracts: Dispater emphasises airtight drafting with no weaknesses, while Mammon is notorious for deals of greed and betrayal. All contracts, in theory, are made under Asmodeus's sufferance—so one might say every infernal contract is indirectly a contract *with* Asmodeus.
:
Practically, you will not deal with archdevils directly unless you reach truly epic stature, but invoking their names lends weight: **"By Mammon's greed, may our contract be profitable,"** or **"In Asmodeus's name, this pact is sealed."** Knowledge of a devil's rank also lets you posture correctly when summoning: lesser devils can be bullied by invoking higher powers, while greater devils must be treated with caution and respect—or bound by exceptionally strong magic.

## Invoking and Negotiating with Infernal Entities

1. **Preparation and Wards.** Prepare a **sanctified summoning circle**: a double circle inscribed with Infernal runes and the sigil of Asmodeus at each cardinal point, drawn in powdered silver and unholy water tainted with a drop of your blood and ash. Inscribe it on stone or hardened brass. It serves as both beacon and prison, functioning as *magic circle*. Ensure no breaks—even a smear is an escape route. Prepare any **relics of authority** you possess; they bolster your legitimacy in the eyes of the summoned.
:
2. **Knowledge and True Names.** Know *whom* you intend to summon, and research its **True Name** if possible. If a true name is out of reach, at minimum know the devil's title, rank, and temperament. Tailor your offer to that knowledge: a contract devil wants an elaborate written instrument to inspect; a bearded devil wants blood and an excuse. **Ignorance is the arch-enemy of the binder.**
:
3. **Invocation Ritual.** Speak the litany firmly: *"By the blood of the covenant and the will of the Ruby Lord, I summon thee, \[Name], from the \[Layer] of Hell. Come forth bound in the name of Asmodeus, bound by the Pact Primeval, to this sacred circle."* Burn a suitable offering—sulfur, myrrh, and a personal token from the one who will deal with the devil.
:
4. **Establish Dominance.** The moment the devil appears, assert control. If you know its true name, use it in a command. Brandish your holy symbol and invoke Asmodeus's authority over all lesser devils. Many devils test the circle immediately. Do *not* ask politely at this stage; **declare** the terms of engagement. Keep a contingency ready: a readied spell, or a fellow cleric hidden nearby with a dismissal prepared.
:
5. **Negotiation.** Devils are master negotiators. Come with a clear objective and boundaries you will not cross. Be precise; offer no more than you must. Always include a clause preventing the devil from harming you or acting against your interests for the pact's duration—*"you shall serve me for one month, doing no harm to me or mine, nor taking action that opposes our agreed goal."* Listen carefully for trickery, and if the devil tries to rush you, slow down. Impatience produces the gaps they live in.
:
6. **Sealing the Deal.** Both parties sign or speak acceptance, then perform the rite of binding: *"Asmodeus, witness and enforce this accord between myself and this servant of Hell. By your authority, it is sealed."* Prick a finger and let blood fall on the contract or into the brazier. The devil is now compelled—as are you.
:
7. **Maintaining Control.** Devils follow the **letter** of the contract and hunt for every unmentioned detail. Do not treat the creature as a friend, but do not taunt it either; there is no profit in giving it a motive for later. Keep the signed instrument on your person, ready to brandish and recite.
:
8. **Termination and Dismissal.** When duties are fulfilled, dismiss formally: *"Our contract is fulfilled. Begone to Hell from whence you came, \[Name], and carry with you our pact's conclusion."* Dispel the circle *after* the devil departs, never before. Cleanse the site, then log the entire event—date, name, terms—in your records. That log is your defence the next time a devil claims you cheated it.

# Wizard: School of Infernal Contracts

*Wizards of this tradition treat spellcasting as the drafting and execution of binding pacts. Each firebolt is signed in flame; each divination is invoked through a devil's witness. They summon fiends, negotiate terms, and bind planar powers to their will—always mindful that every boon from Hell carries its price, and that the price is written in a footnote.*

### Contractual Wizard Features
| Wizard Level | Feature |
|:---:|:---|
| 2nd | Runic Drafting, Contract Sigil |
| 6th | Devil's Retainer, Binding Authority |
| 10th | Master of Covenants |
| 14th | Grand Infernal Binding |

#### 2nd Level: Runic Drafting
You gain proficiency with calligrapher's supplies and learn to read, write, and speak Infernal. You also learn the *fine print* cantrip, which does not count against your number of cantrips known.
:
Your training in infernal law makes your spellbook a legal instrument. When you copy a spell into your spellbook, you can do so in half the usual time and for half the usual cost if the spell is on the Infernal Contract Spell List (Part IX) or has the abjuration, conjuration, or enchantment school.
:
Additionally, when you draft a contract using the Ledger rules, you make the drafting check with advantage.

#### 2nd Level: Contract Sigil
As a bonus action, you can bind yourself or a willing creature you can touch with a **Contract Sigil**—a glowing infernal rune and a whispered clause. As you place the sigil, state one simple **Term**: an action the bearer must take, or must refrain from taking, while the sigil lasts.
:
For 1 hour, once on each of its turns, the bearer can add **1d4** to one damage roll or one saving throw of its choice. If the bearer knowingly violates the Term, the sigil shatters: the effect ends and the bearer takes 2d4 psychic damage.
:
You can have only one sigil active at a time, and you can place a number of sigils equal to your Intelligence modifier (minimum of one) per long rest. Placing a new sigil ends the old one.

> **Flavour.** You trace a blood-red rune in the air, speak *"…per foedus omnia,"* and the mark fuses to flesh or armour—a promise of power drawn against Hell's vault, repayable in ways the bearer has not yet read.

#### 6th Level: Devil's Retainer
Over a 10-minute ritual, you bind a lesser devil into your service as a clerk. It uses the imp stat block, but it cannot make attacks and its Sting attack is replaced by the ability to Help. It obeys your commands, shares its senses with you as a familiar does, and can scribe at your dictation in perfect Infernal.
:
Once per long rest, when a creature you can see within 60 feet of your retainer makes a saving throw against one of your spells, the retainer can **Object**, imposing disadvantage on that save. When it does, everyone within 60 feet hears the objection, whether or not they speak Infernal.
:
If the retainer is reduced to 0 hit points it dissolves into sulfurous paperwork, and you can rebind it after a long rest. Should you dismiss it in violation of its own terms of service, it files a grievance—which is a plot hook, not a mechanic.

#### 6th Level: Binding Authority
When you cast a spell that summons, calls, or binds a fiend, the creature obeys your spoken commands for the spell's duration without requiring a check or a saving throw to control it, provided you have not violated any term you offered it. If you *have* violated such a term, the creature is instead immediately freed and hostile, and it knows exactly which clause you broke.

\page

#### 10th Level: Master of Covenants
Your mastery of infernal law deepens.
:
* **Twin Contracts.** You can maintain two Contract Sigils simultaneously, on different creatures.
* **Superior Drafting.** The Contract Sigil die becomes a **d6**.
* **Warding Covenants.** A creature bearing your sigil gains resistance to fire damage and psychic damage.
* **Consideration.** When a sigil shatters because its bearer violated a Term, you gain temporary hit points equal to twice your wizard level. Hell pays its finder's fees promptly.

#### 14th Level: Grand Infernal Binding
You can cast *contract of infernal servitude* without a spell slot or material components. When you do, you can bind a creature of challenge rating 8 or lower, and the binding does not require concentration.
:
Additionally, whenever you cast a spell that binds, restrains, banishes, or compels a creature whose **True Name** you know, that creature has disadvantage on its saving throw and cannot benefit from legal remedies of any kind, which in practice means it cannot end the effect early by any means short of *wish*.
:
Once you use this feature to cast *contract of infernal servitude* without a slot, you cannot do so again until you finish a long rest.

> **Flavour.** The sky above you darkens as interlocking infernal triangles spin into place, and a bone devil lieutenant steps forth—its service sealed, its objections noted for the record, its contempt entirely professional.

# Paladin: Oath of the Infernal Lord

*"By iron and flame, by blood and dread…"*
:
Paladins who swear this Oath bind soul and steel to Asmodeus himself. Vengeance burns hot in their hearts, but they temper its fury with cold contractual precision, summoning fiendish allies and binding enemies in infernal pacts. They are not brutes. They are process servers with a greatsword.

### Tenets of the Infernal Lord
* **Dominion through Fear.** Inspire dread; enforce Asmodeus's law without mercy.
* **Unbreakable Contract.** Every bargain must be honoured—breaking a pact is a fate worse than death.
* **Temptation's Grace.** Offer infernal power in exchange for weakness; all may be tempted.
* **Merciless Order.** Mercy is chaos; impose structure by any means.
* **Eternal Loyalty.** Your soul, your name, and your will belong wholly to Asmodeus.

### Oath Spells
You gain the following spells at the paladin levels listed. They count as paladin spells for you and do not count against the number of spells you can prepare.

| Paladin Level | Spells |
|:---:|:---|
| 3rd | *hellish rebuke*, *seal of the first pact* |
| 5th | *zone of truth*, *quill of binding* |
| 9th | *magic circle*, *devil's due* |
| 13th | *banishment*, *binding clause* |
| 17th | *flame strike*, *soul covenant* |

#### 3rd Level: Channel Divinity
You gain two Channel Divinity options.
:
**Fiendish Rebuke.** When you or a creature you can see within 10 feet of you takes damage from an attacker you can see within 60 feet, you can use your reaction to present your holy symbol and unleash a bolt of hellfire. The attacker must make a Dexterity saving throw, taking 2d10 fire damage on a failed save, or half as much on a success. This damage increases to 3d10 at 9th level and 4d10 at 15th level.
:
**Fiendish Binding.** As an action, you present your holy symbol and speak an infernal phrase of binding. One creature you can see within 30 feet must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw or be **restrained** by crackling infernal chains for 1 minute. A creature restrained this way can repeat the saving throw at the end of each of its turns, ending the effect on itself on a success. A creature that has broken an oath, contract, or sworn promise within the last 24 hours makes its initial save with disadvantage.

#### 7th Level: Aura of Contract
You and friendly creatures within 10 feet of you have resistance to fire damage.
:
In addition, a creature restrained by your Fiendish Binding cannot teleport, cannot be moved by teleportation or planar travel effects, and cannot be returned to its home plane against your will until the restraint ends.
:
At 18th level, the range of this aura increases to 30 feet.

\page

#### 15th Level: Unbreakable Pact
While you are concentrating on a spell from your Oath Spells list, or maintaining a Channel Divinity effect from this Oath, you cannot be charmed or frightened.
:
Additionally, when a creature affected by one of your Oath spells or by Fiendish Binding repeats a saving throw to end the effect, you can use your reaction to impose disadvantage on that roll. You can do so a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus, regaining all expended uses on a long rest.
:
Finally, you have advantage on saving throws against any effect that would compel you to break a sworn agreement.

#### 20th Level: Avatar of the Infernal Lord
As an action, you assume the true aspect of the Ruby Lord for 1 minute. Horns sprout from your head, your eyes blaze, and an aura of hellfire radiates from you in a 30-foot radius. For the duration:
:
* **Infernal Chains.** A creature of your choice that starts its turn in the aura must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw (DC = 8 + your proficiency bonus + your Charisma modifier) or be restrained until the start of its next turn.
* **Hellfire Blast.** When a creature enters the aura for the first time on a turn, or starts its turn there, it takes 4d6 fire damage.
* **Wings of Flame.** You gain a flying speed equal to your walking speed.
* **Sovereign Authority.** Fiends have disadvantage on saving throws against your spells and Channel Divinity, and you cannot be charmed or frightened.
:
Once you use this feature, you cannot do so again until you finish a long rest.

{{note
##### Playing the Oath Without Playing a Villain
The Oath of the Infernal Lord is written for a lawful character who is genuinely dangerous, not for a player who wants to derail the table. Two guardrails work well in play.
:
**The Contract Is Public.** The paladin's terms are stated aloud and honoured to the letter, including the parts that inconvenience them. A party can plan around a devil they can read.
:
**Enforcement Requires a Breach.** Every punitive feature keys off someone breaking their word first. That is a discipline, not a limitation, and it makes the paladin's restraint the most frightening thing about them.
}}

# Cleric: Contract Domain

*The Contract Domain serves the paperwork of Hell. Its clerics are notaries, auditors, and collections agents—priests who understand that a soul harvested by signature is worth three taken by force, and that the most damning thing you can do to a mortal is give them exactly what they asked for.*
:
Deities of this domain include Asmodeus above all, but also Dispater, Mammon, and any lawful power that governs oaths, debts, and binding words.

### Contract Domain Spells
| Cleric Level | Spells |
|:---:|:---|
| 1st | *seal of the first pact*, *command* |
| 3rd | *quill of binding*, *zone of truth* |
| 5th | *devil's due*, *magic circle* |
| 7th | *binding clause*, *banishment* |
| 9th | *soul covenant*, *geas* |

#### 1st Level: Bonus Proficiencies
You gain proficiency with calligrapher's supplies and heavy armour, and you learn to read, write, and speak Infernal.

#### 1st Level: Notary of the Nine
You can bind a creature to a small promise and make the promise bite.
:
As a bonus action, you offer a **Minor Term** to a creature within 30 feet that can hear and understand you: a single, clearly stated action it must take, or must refrain from taking, before the end of your next turn. The creature must accept willingly.
:
If it accepts, it immediately gains temporary hit points equal to your cleric level. If it accepts and then knowingly breaks the Term while the temporary hit points remain, those hit points vanish and the creature takes psychic damage equal to 1d8 + your Wisdom modifier.
:
You can use this feature a number of times equal to your Wisdom modifier (minimum of once), regaining all uses on a long rest.

#### 2nd Level: Channel Divinity — Writ of Compulsion
As an action, you present your holy symbol and read a clause into the air. One creature within 30 feet that can hear you must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw or be bound by the writ for 1 minute.
:
When you use this feature, name one action the target is forbidden: attacking a specific creature, moving more than 10 feet from its current position, casting spells with verbal components, or leaving an area you designate. Each time the bound creature attempts the forbidden action, the attempt fails, it wastes the action, and it takes **2d10 psychic damage**. It can repeat the saving throw at the end of each of its turns, ending the effect on a success.
:
The damage increases to 3d10 at 8th level and 4d10 at 14th level. Fiends have disadvantage on the initial saving throw; they know exactly who taught you that phrasing.

\page

#### 6th Level: Channel Divinity — Infernal Audit
As an action, you open Hell's books on the world around you. For 1 minute, you know the following about any creature you can see within 60 feet: whether it is bound by an oath, pact, curse, or contract; whether it is currently in breach of one; and the identity of the party holding that instrument if that party is a fiend.
:
While the audit lasts, you can use a bonus action to declare one creature you can see **In Arrears**. Until the audit ends, that creature cannot regain hit points, and the first time on each of your turns that you or an ally deals damage to it, that damage increases by 1d8 necrotic.
:
A creature that is genuinely bound by no agreement of any kind—rare, and worth noticing—is immune to being declared In Arrears.

#### 8th Level: Divine Strike — Hellfire Lien
Once on each of your turns when you hit a creature with a weapon attack, you can cause the attack to deal an extra 1d8 fire damage. When you reach 14th level, this increases to 2d8.
:
If the target is bound by a Minor Term, Writ of Compulsion, contract, or covenant to which you or an ally are a party, the extra damage is necrotic instead of fire, and the target cannot regain hit points until the end of your next turn.

#### 17th Level: Master of the Ledger
Hell recognises your signature.
:
* **Standing Authority.** You cannot be charmed or frightened by fiends, and you have advantage on saving throws against their spells and abilities.
* **Sealed Covenant.** You can cast *soul covenant* once without expending a spell slot or material components. You regain the ability to do so when you finish a long rest.
* **Foreclosure.** When a creature you can see within 60 feet knowingly breaks an oath, contract, or Term to which you are a party or witness, you can use your reaction to call the debt. The creature must make a Charisma saving throw against your spell save DC. On a failed save it takes 8d10 psychic damage and is stunned until the end of its next turn; on a success it takes half as much damage and is not stunned. Once you use this feature, you cannot do so again until you finish a long rest.

{{descriptive
##### Domain Design Notes
The Contract Domain is deliberately conditional. Its best numbers only arrive when someone has agreed to something and then failed to honour it, which means the cleric's strongest play is *social*: extract a promise before the fight, and the fight arrives pre-loaded.
:
Notary of the Nine is the engine. A generous reading lets allies take Terms happily—temporary hit points for agreeing to charge the front line is a good trade for most fighters—so the feature reads as a buff at the table and as a leash in the fiction. Both readings are correct, which is the point.
}}

* **Hellish Boon.** Rather than punish, the clause rewards. A creature that upholds the clause gains the benefit of *bless* whenever it acts in direct service of the contract's purpose, or, at your option, a single-use benefit of advantage on one attack roll, save, or check while so acting.
:
Only one clause effect can be established per casting; a complex contract requires several castings. The caster is immune to the effects of a clause they inscribe, and a clever devil-cleric never signs one from the other side.
:
*Usage in Lore:* High Inquisitors apply Binding Clauses to treaties between mortal nobles under the Church's watch. Entire fiefdoms have fallen when a king broke a Hell-witnessed treaty and found his heirs cursed by the terms. The Scriptorium records famous examples, such as the **Edict of the Everloyal**, which held a mercenary company to a hundred days' service under pain of transformation into slugs.

### Soul Covenant
###### *5th-level enchantment (infernal)*
###### **Casting Time:** 1 minute
###### **Range:** 15 feet
###### **Components:** V, S, M (diamond dust worth 200 gp and a sliver of a soul coin, both consumed)
###### **Duration:** Until fulfilled or broken
###### **Classes:** Cleric, Paladin, Warlock, Wizard
:
You speak aloud a covenant, binding up to two willing creatures—which may include yourself—into a soul-bound contract beneath Hell's regard. All parties must be within range for the full casting, and all must agree to the declared terms before it completes. As you finish, each signatory's name burns itself into the air in fiery script and brands onto the covenant document. Each creature bears an **Infernal Mark** thereafter, signifying that its soul is pledged to the deal.
:
While the covenant persists, all parties are subject to the following:
:
* **Bond of Obligation.** Each creature is under a *geas* effect with no time limit, compelled to fulfil the agreed terms. Ignoring the duty or acting counter to it inflicts 5d10 psychic damage per day of defiance. A willful attempt to break the covenant outright engulfs the creature's mark in hellfire, dealing 8d6 fire damage immediately as a warning. This effect cannot be ended by *remove curse* or *dispel magic*; only *wish*, divine intervention, or fulfilment of the terms will end it.
:
* **Shared Fate (Optional Clause).** You can declare at casting that the signatories' fates are linked. If so, they cannot directly harm one another until the covenant resolves—attacks are stayed by an invisible force, harmful spells fizzle—and if one party dies before the terms are met, each survivor takes 10d6 necrotic damage as part of the forfeit is extracted from them.
:
* **Infernal Witness.** The covenant is recorded in the grand infernal ledgers the instant it is sealed. Any fiend of Intelligence 6 or higher recognises a marked creature as under contract and will not casually interfere with it. You have **advantage on Charisma checks** made to influence lawful evil fiends in matters relevant to the covenant.
:
**Breach.** If a party grossly violates the terms, the covenant exacts payment at once: the offender is struck as by *flame strike* (6d6 fire and 6d6 radiant, the radiance being the searing light of Hell's judgment) and its soul is forfeit to the Nine Hells. A character whose soul is claimed this way is beyond ordinary resurrection and, in most campaigns, beyond further play; discuss this with your GM before signing anything.
:
*Sacred and Scorned Use:* This is the apex of infernal contract magic accessible to mortal casters—a miniature Pact Primeval. The Church reserves it for alliances between high priests, the sanctification of dark knighthoods, and the securing of a powerful warlock's soul. The casting is a spectacle: black candles, chanting choirs, lesser devils in attendance, diamond dust flaring in a brazier as each name burns. When the marks brand onto flesh, participants report searing pain followed by euphoria, as though touched directly by Asmodeus. Few dare break such a covenant; the known instances are infamous and are recited as morality plays by Hell's standards.

### Amend the Record

# Appendix B: Running the Church

{{wide
Six seeds for GMs, each built on the Ledger and the templates rather than on combat.
}}

**The Free Trial.** A Chartered Protector offers the party three journeys of infernal protection at no charge. There is no catch in Articles I through V. The catch is the Emergency Invocation Clause, and the ambush waiting on the fourth journey was arranged by the same Church.
:
**The Audit.** A bone devil arrives in town with a ledger and a writ, and begins auditing every oath sworn there in the last century—including a marriage vow, a knighthood, and a debt the party's patron would very much prefer stayed unexamined. It is not hostile. It is thorough.
:
**Class Action.** Three hundred villagers signed identical Covenants of Perilous Passage. One clause was drafted badly. The party can void all three hundred contracts at once if they can find the defect and argue it—or a rival firm of infernal advocates can find it first and sell it back to the Church.
:
**The Substitution.** A dying noble at 95% tithe offers the party a fortune to find someone willing to substitute for the remainder. There are volunteers. That is the problem.
:
**Golgorath's Quill.** The Scriptorium's artifact quill has been stolen. Whoever holds it can draft a contract that Hell must recognise—including a contract Hell would never willingly file.
:
**Amendment.** Someone has been quietly casting *amend the record* on the founding charter of a city, one Article per decade, for two hundred years. The city's laws now say something no living person intended. The auditor is a century behind.
