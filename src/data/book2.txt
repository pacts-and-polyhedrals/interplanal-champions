Book II: Player’s Guide to the Championship of Infernal Succession
An original level-12-to-17 D&D 5e campaign supplement for The Throne of Avernus. Companion to Book I and The 
Night of the Red Clause.
Audience: Players and DMs. Starting level: 12. Ending level: approximately 17. Rules baseline: 2014-era D&D 5e 
with a 2024 conversion note. Contents: championship charter, three Asmodean subclasses, 17 new spells, 14 new 
magic items, eight printable contractual forms, six level-12 pregenerated claimants, and player-facing orientation.
What kind of campaign is this?
Asmodeus has opened a new Rite of Succession for the First Layer of Hell. Challengers from many planes may 
compete for the throne of Avernus. Victory requires more than violence: contestants must demonstrate survival, 
clever legal manoeuvring, and the ability to win consent and command. The prize is real. A sole regent may rule; a 
declared allied slate may share power; a contestant may represent a patron; anyone may withdraw subject to the 
specific terms they signed.
You are not required to be evil, to sell your soul, to betray your friends, or to fight other player characters. The 
tournament invites all of those possibilities but makes none compulsory. Your choices can remake Avernus, reinforce 
its present horrors, or expose a scheme that neither the lords of Hell nor their challengers fully understand.
What characters know before entering
• The tournament is an act of Asmodeus, whose sovereignty over the Nine Hells is unchanged. The successful challenger wins 
authority over Avernus, not Hell as a whole.
• Each claimant must prove Survival, Deviousness, and Dominion. Strength can qualify, but honourable ingenuity, negotiation 
and stewardship also count.
• Written agreements matter. Read every article. Contracts are public game objects with actual terms and remedies.
• The Brass Arbiter hears appeals. It can reject improper collection, forged signatures and unauthorised proxy bargains.
• There is a mechanism for individual, joint and proxy claims. Decide what your character actually wants before you arrive.
1. THE CHAMPIONSHIP CHARTER
The Nine Articles of Infernal Succession
This is the real player-facing charter. It can be handed out and used at the table. In the fiction it is witnessed by the 
Office of the Ninth Crown. No DM-only appendix changes these terms without a visible amendment and the 
genuine acceptance of an affected signatory.

Article I - Notice and Acceptance. A contender enters only after freely accepting this Charter, personally or through 
a disclosed and authorised proxy. Participation does not by itself pledge a soul, bloodline, descendants, memories or 
previously held property. Contestants who decline are not subject to its competitive obligations.
Article II - Competence. Qualification for final investiture requires valid proof of Survival, Deviousness, and 
Dominion. Competence may be demonstrated by physical contest, tactical leadership, investigation, negotiation, 
contract drafting, or other witnessed acts relevant to the Proof. No particular alignment or class is required.
Article III - Form of Claim. A claimant may seek the office alone, as part of a joint slate, or as the disclosed 
representative of a patron. A joint slate is one legal claimant for eligibility and reward purposes, with several named 
members. It must publish its internal voting and succession rules before investiture.
Article IV - Consent and Persons. No soul, memory, identity, body, service beyond the explicit competition, or 
interest in a dependent is transferred by acceptance of this Charter. Additional bargains require separate, informed 
and specific acceptance by the person holding the right being transferred.
Article V - Evidence and Eligibility. Certified records, witnessed acts, magical verification and authentic signatures 
are admissible. Forged entries may be challenged. A claimant has a right to review allegations against their eligibility 
and to present counterevidence before exclusion.
Article VI - Hearings. The Brass Arbiter is independent of individual contestants and must state the clause upon 
which each ruling relies. An appeal submitted before a scheduled forfeiture stays that forfeiture until the hearing, 
except immediate defensive action during active combat.
Article VII - Deceit and Contest. Tactical deception, disguise, misdirection, negotiation, coercive battlefield magic 
within the agreed contest, and lawful exploitation of a clause are permissible. Forgery of the Arbiter’s seal, false 
representation of another person’s consent, and threats against uninvolved dependents are not. A successful combat 
feint does not transform into an agreement to transfer a soul.
Article VIII - Duties and Withdrawal. A contestant may withdraw before final investiture and depart via a 
sanctioned return route once any particular, previously signed service duties are discharged. Withdrawal forfeits 
unspent Crown Marks but incurs no new soul penalty. A crowned ruler must defend the First Layer against invasion, 
uphold the published governance charter and answer the superior authority of the Lord of Nessus.
Article IX - Investiture. The lawful victor obtains the First Crown and the ability to appoint ministers, issue civil 
decrees and govern Avernus subject to Asmodeus’s superior sovereignty and existing higher infernal law. The prize 
may be held by a registered joint slate. Investiture is witnessed publicly by the Brass Arbiter; Asmodeus will not 
withdraw it solely because he dislikes a lawful outcome.
Player’s legal quick start
When arguing an objection, identify (1) the named contract, (2) the actual clause, (3) who owns the right, (4) the 
evidence of consent or default, and (5) your requested remedy. A well-substantiated factual contradiction may 
succeed without a roll. When an issue is genuinely disputed, the DM should announce its legal DC rather than hiding 
an unanswerable rule.
Three claims you can make
My crown: You will rule alone, rewarding allies with offices or granting them independence. Our crown: Sign the 
Covenant of Joint Regency to govern together, with the ability to disagree under agreed rules. Their crown: You 
represent an NPC or patron; declare who that is and what you will personally receive.

2. NEW SUBCLASS - WARLOCK: THE RUBY COURT
Otherworldly Patron, 2014 warlock subclass features at levels 1, 6, 10 and 14. For 2024 warlock, grant the 1st-level 
patron feature at level 3 along with the expanded patron spells; later levels are unchanged. This subclass is separate 
from the pre-existing Fiend patron and can be selected instead of it.
Warlocks of the Ruby Court bargain for the authority to witness, suspend and enforce specific promises. They may 
work for Asmodeus, copy his methods without loyalty, or use Hell’s law against Hell itself. Their magic cannot 
conjure soul ownership from an enemy who has not agreed to it.
Expanded Patron Spells (2014): 1st command, sanctuary; 2nd zone of truth, hold person; 3rd counterspell, sending; 
4th banishment, freedom of movement; 5th geas, wall of force. These are options on the warlock spell list, not free 
extra known spells. Under a 2024 approach, treat these as always prepared patron spells if the table uses that method 
for warlocks.
Level 1: Clause of Mutual Aid
You learn Infernal and gain proficiency in Investigation or Persuasion. As a bonus action, you may offer one willing 
creature you can see within 30 ft a Minor Term that is a feasible, clearly stated action to perform or avoid before the 
start of your next turn. The creature knows the entire effect and may refuse without consequence. While it accepts and 
abides by the Term, once before it expires the creature may add 1d6 to one attack roll, saving throw or ability check 
after rolling but before the outcome is announced. If it knowingly breaks the Term, the unused die disappears; there is 
no involuntary soul loss or damage. You may offer terms a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus per long 
rest; only one of your terms may be active at a time. This is neither concentration nor a spell.
Level 6: Consideration Received
When a hostile creature that has separately accepted a Minor Term from you knowingly breaks it within 60 ft, you 
can use a reaction to deal 2d8 psychic damage and gain temporary hit points equal to your Charisma modifier plus 
your proficiency bonus. You may use this reaction a number of times equal to your Charisma modifier (minimum 
once) per long rest, and no creature can trigger it more than once per long rest. Friendly characters never trigger this 
damage through playful self-default: the feature targets a hostile participant. In addition, you have advantage on 
ability checks to locate a deliberate contradiction in an authentic written agreement you have been allowed to inspect 
for 10 minutes; this does not substitute for the actual evidence needed to win a hearing.
Level 10: Reserved Rights
You gain resistance to psychic damage and advantage on saves against being charmed or frightened by fiends. Once 
per long rest, when you fail a saving throw against a spell or magical effect, you may use your reaction to reroll it. 
You must use the second result. The reroll is represented as a timely objection, not retrospective refusal to accept an 
already completed lawful bargain.
Level 14: Final Notice
As an action, choose a creature within 60 ft that can hear or see you. It makes a Charisma saving throw against your 
warlock spell save DC. On a failure, it takes 6d10 psychic damage and is restrained in spectral filing chains for up to 
1 minute. It repeats the save at the end of each turn, ending the restraint on success. On a success, it takes half damage 
and is not restrained. If the target is currently bound by a genuinely accepted Minor Term from you and knowingly 
breached it, the initial damage increases by 2d10. Once used, this feature cannot be used again until a long rest. This 
is a magical attack, not an actual contract granting your patron title to a soul.

Design ceiling: Short-lived attack/save support, one optional control ultimate, no automatic mind control, no extra 
spell slots and no involuntary soul transfer. The subclass remains a caster and negotiator, not a second full cleric.
3. NEW SUBCLASS - BARBARIAN: PATH OF THE BLOOD-PRICE
Primal Path at levels 3, 6, 10, 14 for both 2014 and 2024-style subclass timing.
These warriors swear measurable combat vows and draw power from the peril of keeping them. A Blood-Price 
barbarian might defend innocent people out of fierce devotion or serve Asmodeus as an honour-bound executioner.
Level 3: Rage Covenant
When you enter a Rage, speak one Battle Vow whose condition you can reasonably fulfil: protect a named willing 
ally within 30 ft, hold a location you can see, or engage a declared enemy without deliberately fleeing. The vow lasts 
until your Rage ends. On each of your turns while raging, if you have not knowingly broken it, the first creature you 
hit with a Strength-based melee weapon attack takes an extra 1d6 necrotic or fire damage (your choice when you 
vow). This increases to 1d8 at barbarian level 10. If you knowingly break the vow, you lose this extra damage until 
you start a new Rage; your Rage itself does not end. The vow does not magically force your actions or bind any ally’s 
soul.
Level 6: Collateral Guardian
While raging, when a willing ally within 5 ft is damaged by an attack, you can use your reaction to reduce the damage 
by 1d10 + your Constitution modifier + your Rage Damage bonus. You take psychic damage equal to half the 
damage prevented, rounded down; this damage cannot be reduced by Rage resistance. You may use this feature a 
number of times equal to your proficiency bonus per long rest. The ally knows you are assuming the cost and may 
decline the intervention.
Level 10: Witness to the Oath
When you begin Rage Covenant, you may designate one willing ally within 30 ft as a Witness. While your Rage lasts 
and you are conscious, that ally has advantage on saving throws against being frightened. Once during each Rage, 
when the Witness misses an attack against the foe named by your Battle Vow, the Witness may add your Rage 
Damage bonus to the attack roll, potentially turning the miss into a hit. The Witness cannot gain this benefit while 
incapacitated, and may withdraw from the designation at any time.
Level 14: The Price Comes Due
While raging, when a hostile creature within 30 ft damages your designated Witness or an ally named by your Battle 
Vow, you may use your reaction to move up to half your speed toward that enemy without provoking opportunity 
attacks and make one melee weapon attack against it, provided it comes within reach. On a hit, deal an extra 2d8 
necrotic damage. You may use this feature a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus per long rest. You 
cannot use it during the same reaction as Collateral Guardian.
Design ceiling: Expected extra Rage Covenant damage resembles established barbarian bonus-damage subclasses; 
reaction defence trades personal HP and an action-economy slot for protection. The oath’s penalty is limited and 
disclosed.

4. NEW SUBCLASS - DRUID: CIRCLE OF ASHEN COVENANTS
Druid Circle at levels 2, 6, 10 and 14 (2014). For 2024 druids, grant the level-2 features at level 3 and follow the 
table’s revised Wild Shape charges.
The Circle teaches that every harvest carries a debt, every flame nourishes ash, and every oath must have a season in 
which it ends. Some druids venerate Asmodeus as an embodiment of cosmic exchange. Others study his contracts 
precisely to keep natural communities out of them.
Level 2: Witness of the Grove
You gain proficiency in Insight or Nature, and learn Infernal or Sylvan. As a 1-minute rite, you may place an Ashen 
Accord on one willing creature you touch. It lasts until your next long rest, until dismissed by either party (no action), 
or until another Ashen Accord replaces it. While within 60 ft of you, when the bearer takes damage you may use your 
reaction to reduce the damage by 1d8 + your Wisdom modifier and grant the bearer temporary hit points equal to 
your proficiency bonus. You may use the reduction a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus per long rest; 
no creature is compelled to accept it. A promise to help the grove is the roleplaying consideration, not an enforceable 
property interest.
Level 2: Ash Warden Spirit
As a bonus action you can expend one use of Wild Shape to manifest an Ash Warden Spirit in an unoccupied space 
within 30 ft for 10 minutes. Its statistics: Small Elemental; AC 13 + your proficiency bonus; HP 5 + five times your 
druid level; speed 30 ft, fly 20 ft (hover); fire resistance; immune poisoned condition; attack bonus equal to your spell 
attack modifier. It acts on your initiative immediately after your turn. It can move and Dodge freely; if you spend a 
bonus action to command it, it instead moves and makes one Ashen Touch attack (1d8 + your proficiency bonus fire 
damage), Helps, or Dashes. It cannot cast spells, carry large objects or make opportunity attacks. It vanishes at 0 HP, 
when you dismiss it, or when the duration expires. You cannot have more than one Ash Warden active. At druid level 
10 its Ashen Touch deals 2d8 + PB instead.
Level 6: Remedy of the Root
When a creature you can see within 60 ft harms the bearer of your Ashen Accord, you can use your reaction to 
impose a -1d6 penalty on one attack roll that creature makes before the end of its next turn; the penalty must be 
declared before that roll. If you use this reaction, the Accord bearer may immediately move 5 ft without provoking 
opportunity attacks. You may use the reaction a number of times equal to your Wisdom modifier (minimum once) per 
long rest. It competes with Witness of the Grove for your reaction.
Level 10: Covenant of Embers
You gain fire resistance. When your Ash Warden Spirit is within 30 ft of the bearer of your Ashen Accord, the bearer 
has advantage on saving throws against being frightened. When the spirit scores a critical hit, its target cannot make 
opportunity attacks until the end of the target’s next turn. No stacking of duplicate spirit bonuses.
Level 14: Court of Returning Ash
As an action, you raise a 30-foot-radius grove of black trees centred on yourself for up to 1 minute, as if concentrating 
on a spell. You may include or exclude any willing creatures you can see when you activate it. For the duration, the 
first time each round that an included creature takes damage within the area, reduce that damage by 2d8. An enemy 
that starts its turn within the grove must make a Wisdom save against your spell save DC or have its speed halved 
until the start of its next turn. The grove is stationary and counts as difficult terrain for enemies; it ends if your 

concentration ends. Once used, you cannot use this feature again until a long rest. It does not nullify valid signed 
agreements or compel an enemy to contract.
Design ceiling: The spirit’s attack requires a bonus action and does not combine with repeated Wild Shape strikes; 
the reactive mitigation competes for the same reaction; the capstone uses concentration. The druid remains an 
effective full caster.

Witness Mark
Divination cantrip. Cast: 1 action. Range: Touch. Components: V, S. Duration: 1 hour. Classes: Bard, Cleric, 
Druid, Warlock, Wizard. You mark a willing creature or a document with a faint sigil. While the mark lasts, you can 
recognise the marked target if you see it within 60 ft, even if superficially disguised. The mark gives no truesight and 
does not defeat invisibility. Once before the spell ends, the bearer can choose to reveal the sigil to gain advantage on 
an ability check to verify the authenticity of its own statement or a signature it personally made. The sigil then 
vanishes. You can have only one Witness Mark active.
Ember Receipt
Evocation cantrip. Cast: 1 action. Range: 60 ft. Components: V, S. Duration: Instantaneous. Classes: Druid, 
Sorcerer, Warlock, Wizard. Make a ranged spell attack against a creature. On a hit it takes 1d8 fire damage; if it 
knowingly broke a clearly stated promise to you during the previous round, it takes an additional 1d4 fire damage (no 
additional damage from merely signing a contract). Base damage becomes 2d8 at character level 5, 3d8 at 11 and 4d8 
at 17; the bonus stays 1d4.

Formal Objection
1st-level abjuration. Cast: 1 reaction, when a creature within range makes a saving throw against being charmed or 
frightened. Range: 60 ft. Components: V. Duration: Instantaneous. Classes: Bard, Cleric, Paladin, Warlock, 
Wizard. Before the DM announces the outcome of the save, add 1d4 to the roll. The spell cannot retroactively undo 
an event. Higher slots: Add another 1d4 at slot levels 3, 5 and 7, to a maximum of 4d4.
Promissory Ward
1st-level abjuration. Cast: 1 reaction, when a willing creature within range is damaged by an attack. Range: 30 ft. 
Components: V, S. Duration: Instantaneous. Classes: Cleric, Druid, Paladin, Warlock. Reduce the triggering 
damage by 1d10 + your spellcasting ability modifier. The protected creature may freely promise to aid you later, but 
this promise has no automatic magical penalty. Higher slots: Add 1d10 reduction per slot level above 1st.
Registry of Names
2nd-level divination (ritual). Cast: 10 minutes. Range: Touch. Components: V, S, M (a quill and indexed paper 
worth 10 gp, not consumed). Duration: Instantaneous. Classes: Bard, Cleric, Druid, Warlock, Wizard. Touch a 
named document. Learn whether up to six listed signatures were made by the people they purport to represent, 
whether the writing has been magically altered since signing, and whether any named signatory appears twice under 
mutually incompatible identities. You do not learn true names, a hidden signatory’s location, or what a lawful 
contract means. The spell examines the document, not every copy in the universe.
Collateral Chains
2nd-level conjuration (concentration, up to 1 minute). Cast: 1 action. Range: 60 ft. Components: V, S. Classes: 
Sorcerer, Warlock, Wizard. One creature you see must make a Strength save. On failure, spectral brass chains restrain 
it. It may repeat the save at the end of each of its turns, ending the spell on a success. The chains end early if you lose 
concentration. A creature restrained by this spell is not legally indebted to you.
Writ of Safe Passage
3rd-level abjuration (ritual). Cast: 10 minutes. Range: Touch. Components: V, S, M (a brass token worth 25 gp, not 
consumed). Duration: 1 hour. Classes: Cleric, Druid, Paladin, Warlock, Wizard. Up to six willing creatures who 
touch the token when cast gain advantage on their first saving throw each against an environmental hazard caused by 
heat, extreme cold, acidic rain or planar instability during the duration. Each creature also knows the shortest 
nonmagical path back to the casting location while within 1 mile. The spell neither protects from weapon attacks nor 
compels a planar authority to grant entry.
Duel by Covenant
3rd-level abjuration (concentration, up to 1 minute). Cast: 1 action. Range: 60 ft. Components: V, S. Classes: Bard, 
Cleric, Paladin, Warlock. Name two willing creatures within range (one may be you) who can communicate their 
agreement. For the duration, each duellist gains +1 AC against attacks made by creatures other than the other duellist, 
and each can once use a reaction to move 10 ft without provoking opportunity attacks from outsiders. The spell ends 
for both if either duellist withdraws consent (no action), if they move more than 120 ft apart, or if concentration ends. 
It does not force them to fight and does not cancel legal duels already established.
Custody of Memory
4th-level abjuration (concentration, up to 1 hour). Cast: 1 action. Range: Touch. Components: V, S, M (a clear 
glass bead worth 50 gp, not consumed). Classes: Bard, Cleric, Druid, Warlock, Wizard. One willing creature gains 

advantage on Intelligence and Wisdom saving throws against spells and effects that erase, modify or extract its 
memories. Once during the duration, when it would fail such a save, it may choose to succeed instead; doing so ends 
the spell. The spell has no effect on a memory already voluntarily transferred by a valid signed contract.
Breach of Dominion
4th-level transmutation. Cast: 1 action. Range: 90 ft. Components: V, S. Duration: Instantaneous. Classes: Druid, 
Sorcerer, Warlock, Wizard. A construct or fiend within range makes a Constitution save. On failure, it takes 5d8 force 
damage and cannot take reactions until the end of its next turn; on success, half damage and no other effect. A 
construct currently being compelled by a magical command can immediately make an additional saving throw against 
that command if one was initially allowed. Higher slots: +1d8 damage per slot above 4th.
Temporary Injunction
5th-level abjuration (concentration, up to 1 minute). Cast: 1 action. Range: 60 ft. Components: V, S. Classes: 
Cleric, Paladin, Warlock, Wizard. One creature you can see makes a Charisma save. On failure, while within 60 ft of 
you it cannot teleport or use a feature to summon another creature. It repeats the save at the end of each turn, ending 
the spell on success. On initial success, nothing happens. This does not prohibit ordinary movement, existing 
companions or a creature’s ability to leave the area on foot. Teleportation that is part of a mythic death-and-rebirth 
feature is suppressed only if the creature fails the save and the feature does not explicitly override spells.
Audit the False
5th-level divination (ritual). Cast: 10 minutes. Range: Touch. Components: V, S, M (ink dusted with platinum 
worth 100 gp, consumed). Duration: Instantaneous. Classes: Bard, Cleric, Warlock, Wizard. Touch one signed 
document or active magical record. You learn which of up to ten named signatures are forged or produced under 
magical impersonation; the date and general kind of magical alteration; and whether any cited authority belongs to the 
purported issuer. You do not automatically learn the true perpetrator, and the spell cannot declare a genuinely 
voluntary but harmful agreement invalid. A creature capable of concealing its identity from divination may oppose 
you with a Charisma save against your spell save DC; on success, the spell reveals that this entry is obscured, not 
that it is authentic.
Sever Assigned Name
6th-level abjuration. Cast: 1 action. Range: 60 ft. Components: V, S, M (a seal of genuine testimony worth 250 gp, 
consumed). Duration: Instantaneous. Classes: Cleric, Druid, Warlock, Wizard. Choose a willing creature whose 
identity or name has been assigned to a magical claimant without their permission. One spell or magical effect of 6th 
level or lower sustaining that false assignment ends. For a higher-level effect, make a spellcasting ability check 
against DC 10 + the effect’s level, similar to a dispelling contest. If a creature is actively sustaining the assignment, it 
may make a Charisma save; on success, only the assignment’s control is suppressed until the end of your next turn. 
This does not erase a legitimate name, make a true soul bargain disappear, or kill a person sustained by magic.
Planar Reprieve
6th-level conjuration. Cast: 1 minute. Range: Touch. Components: V, S, M (a consecrated home-key worth 500 gp, 
not consumed). Duration: Instantaneous. Classes: Cleric, Druid, Warlock, Wizard. You and up to five willing 
creatures touching you teleport to a circle that you personally registered earlier on the same plane and within 10 
miles. You must have spent 1 hour preparing that circle. This spell cannot escape a sealed plane and is blocked by 
effects that prevent teleportation. It is deliberately slower and shorter-range than a universal emergency escape.

Sovereign Injunction
7th-level enchantment (concentration, up to 1 minute). Cast: 1 action. Range: 60 ft. Components: V, S. Classes: 
Bard, Cleric, Warlock, Wizard. One creature that can understand any language you speak makes a Charisma save. On 
failure, choose one: it cannot make opportunity attacks; it cannot use legendary actions; or it cannot teleport. At the 
end of each of its turns it may repeat the save, ending the injunction on success. A target with Legendary Resistance 
may use it normally. The spell does not prevent ordinary actions, remove legendary resistances or compel a contract. 
This spell may fail against an especially potent sovereign creature even when a weaker foe would be bound.
Writ of Provisional Dawn
7th-level transmutation (concentration, up to 10 minutes). Cast: 1 action. Range: 120 ft (60-ft-radius sphere). 
Components: V, S, M (a sun-marked hourglass worth 500 gp, not consumed). Classes: Cleric, Druid, Warlock, 
Wizard. Within the sphere, magical effects that rely on a false or frozen local time instead use the actual passing time 
at the effect’s original place of jurisdiction, if that place is known to the caster. This cannot accelerate time, force true 
sunlight, end a legitimate contract before its term, or negate the passage of a real night. Any creature sustaining a 
false-time effect may make a Charisma save; on success, its effect is unaffected until the start of your next turn, at 
which point the sphere attempts again.
Statute of the First Crown
8th-level abjuration (concentration, up to 1 hour). Cast: 1 minute. Range: Self (100-ft-radius sphere). Components: 
V, S, M (a bronze crown replica worth 1,000 gp, not consumed). Classes: Cleric, Warlock (8th-level Mystic 
Arcanum), Wizard. Declare a single procedural protection that applies equally to all creatures within the sphere: (a) 
no magical effect may use a forged signature as a source of authority; (b) no unwilling creature can be compelled to 
testify by magical effects of 5th level or lower; or (c) contracts of 5th level or lower cannot be magically altered while 
within the sphere. A hostile magical creature whose effect would be stopped may make a Charisma save against your 
spell save DC when first affected, allowing its particular effect to continue for one round on success. The protection 
does not invalidate truthful testimony, a genuine signed contract, or a superior divine law. The sphere is stationary. 
Casting this spell does not itself make you ruler of Avernus.
Spell use and stacking
New spell damage is not increased merely by using an infernal title. Multiple instances of the same spell do not stack. 
A character can concentrate on only one concentration effect. A warlock learns these spells under the standard class 
rules: the 8th-level spell requires a Mystic Arcanum selection, not a normal Pact Magic slot. The spells can be found 
as researched scrolls during the campaign; DMs need not grant every spell automatically.

Wondrous item, uncommon. A signature written with this quill leaves an indelible, visible timestamp and the signer’s

HP. The gauntlets never create extra Crown Marks; they only reward the underlying action.

Wondrous item, rare; three pairs. These are reusable restraints for Medium or smaller creatures. A willing creature 
can be bound automatically; an unwilling, grappled creature is bound only after an action and a successful DC 17 
Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) check contested by its Athletics or Acrobatics. A restrained wearer 
can use its action to attempt DC 17 Athletics or Acrobatics to escape. The manacles prevent nonmagical use of hands 
but do not suppress spellcasting by themselves. Once per long rest, the wielder can activate them to prevent the 

wearer from teleporting until the end of its next turn on a failed DC 17 Charisma save. They contain no soul-
ownership clause.
Marshal’s Chain of Allegiance
Wondrous item, rare (requires attunement). When a willing creature within 30 feet explicitly accepts your command 
for a specific task of no more than 1 minute, you may link a spectral brass strand to it; you can have only one such 
link active. Once per short rest, when the linked creature fails a saving throw, you can use your reaction to add 1d6 to 
the failed roll, potentially turning it into a success. Either participant can end the link instantly without cost. This is 
teamwork, not domination.
Ashen Seed of Remembrance
Wondrous item, rare (requires attunement by a spellcaster). Planting this dark-red seed over 1 minute creates a 20-
foot-radius thicket lasting 1 hour. Willing creatures resting within it have advantage on checks and saving throws 
against magical memory alteration for the duration. Once per long rest, one creature completing a short rest in the 
thicket may restore one expended Hit Die and can remember one event from its own life that had been altered by a 
spell of 5th level or lower, subject to the spell’s original saving throw if applicable. It cannot recreate another 
person’s experiences or restore a soul already lawfully sold.
Glassheart Interpreter
Wondrous item, rare. A palm-sized receiver keyed to Janella Greenbottle’s maker-mark. Holding it within 30 feet of 
one of her golems allows a creature proficient in Arcana or tinker’s tools to hear the construct’s current orders and 
detect whether they were altered after leaving her workshop. Once per long rest, the operator can use an action to 
give a construct another saving throw against a magical command effect, or force an uncontrolled construct to make 
DC 17 Wisdom save or lose reactions until its next turn. The device never grants permanent ownership or control of 
an unwilling autonomous construct.
Pyric Covenant Ring
Ring, rare (requires attunement). You gain resistance to necrotic damage. Once per long rest, when a creature within 
30 feet willingly tells you a truthful fact that would place it at risk, you can use a reaction to grant it 2d8 temporary 
HP and advantage on its next saving throw before the start of your next turn. The ring’s inscription reads: A secret 
given freely is worth more than a secret stolen. A rival who sees the ring may assume you work for Misty; the ring 
itself does not bind you to her.
Contract-Splitter Blade
Weapon (any sword), very rare (requires attunement). A +2 magic sword. Once on each of your turns when you hit a 
creature maintaining a magical contract effect, you can deal 1d6 additional force damage. Once per long rest, after 
hitting a target, you may choose one magical effect of 5th level or lower currently imposing a penalty or compulsion 
on that target; make a DC 17 ability check using your spellcasting ability, or Intelligence if you have none. On 
success, end that effect. This cannot cancel a valid written agreement itself; it severs one active magical enforcement 
effect.
Gavel of Appeal
Wondrous item, very rare (requires attunement by a spellcaster). This blackwood gavel has 3 charges, regaining 1d3 
daily. As an action expend one charge to cast dispel magic at 4th level. When resolving a public, contested hearing 
with documents that the relevant adjudicator can inspect, you can expend one charge to grant advantage on one 

Investigation, Religion or Persuasion check concerning admissibility or interpretation. No more than one charge may 
be spent this way per hearing, and the gavel cannot turn a lie into a true exhibit.
Writshield of Personal Sovereignty
Shield, very rare (requires attunement). A +1 shield. When you or a creature within 10 feet fails a Charisma saving 
throw against a magical attempt to alter identity, bind a soul or compel a personal oath, you can use your reaction to 
allow a reroll. The second result stands. This can be done twice per long rest. During the Heartstone confrontation, 
the shield can serve as one authentic protection against the queen’s identity-seizure effects, but gives no automatic 
immunity to her spells.
Hourglass of True Dawn
Wondrous item, very rare (requires attunement). Sand inside the glass flows according to time on the plane and 
jurisdiction named when the hourglass is activated. Once per long rest, as an action, create a 30-foot-radius sphere 
centred on the glass for 1 minute. Magical environmental effects relying solely on fabricated or frozen time are 
suppressed within the sphere. A hostile source may make a DC 18 Charisma save when the field appears; on success, 
its effect is delayed rather than suppressed for one round. The sphere does not create actual daylight, erase legitimate 
service, or halt natural time. In Misty’s lair, activating it can delay one lair-action pulse or the Heartstone recovery 
pulse, not both.
Lantern of the Three Witnesses
Wondrous item, very rare (requires attunement). Three oil reservoirs are inscribed Name, Consent, and Jurisdiction. 
The lantern has three total charges and cannot be recharged until the campaign’s current Heartstone rite ends. One 
charge makes a hidden true-name inscription visible for 10 minutes; another grants advantage on one saving throw 
against a hag’s enchantment or illusion (declare before rolling); the third illuminates a hidden soul-anchor or its 
nearest ward when within 60 feet. A bearer who has gathered all three Heartstone witness statements may use all 
remaining charges as an action to deactivate one of the queen’s three witness-seals. It does not deal damage to Misty.
Blackthorn Shears
Wondrous item, very rare. A single-use ritual implement shaped from living infernal blackthorn. With an action while 
adjacent to a properly exposed soul-anchor, cut the magical tether connecting the anchor to its owner. This succeeds 
only if the party has already disabled two witness-seals or obtained a valid independent court order establishing 
unlawful ownership. If used on Misty’s Heartstone after her second defeat, the shears destroy the tether and render 
the stone vulnerable to physical destruction. They cannot permanently kill a creature while its living body is still 
elsewhere or erase a consented mortal contract as an incidental benefit.
Equipment balance and attunement
For a standard four-to-six-person party, three to five of these permanent attunement items by level 15 and three 
Heartstone tools spread among different characters by level 17 provide meaningful preparation without assuming 
every player gets a new legendary weapon. The unique anti-Misty tools help the party; they do not make a CR 30 
mythic encounter safe. Item features calling for an action, reaction, charge or concentration use that action economy 
exactly.

Why Avernus? Nema does not want Hell to be good; she wants it to stop using lies as a pretext for cruelty. She 
considers the First Crown an office whose holder can actually be held to account.
Private pressure: Her monastery once accepted a bargain to protect its novices. The bargain may be valid but 
harmful. Nema wants a solution that honours the signer while freeing those who never consented.
Claim: Sole—create a charter of protected persons; Joint—independent moral auditor; Proxy—sponsor a pact-bound 
order of guardians.
Tactics: Prioritise spirit guardians in close fights, bless when saving throws are more important than area damage, 
and use healing word to rescue fallen allies. During hearings, zone of truth and authentic records complement rather 
than replace direct testimony.
Level-17 direction: Cleric 17 gains 9th-level magic and the domain’s highest feature; focus on lawful restitution and 
protection from coercive identity magic.
Shared party introductions
Start the campaign with each player answering two questions: What would you change about Avernus if you could 
actually hold the First Crown? and Which personal principle would you refuse to trade, even to win? Then have 
them choose one ordinary noninfernal tie to another pregen: saving a life, sharing a mentor, a prior Waterdeep 
hearing, or a debt already paid. No one begins required to betray another player. If PCs prefer separate claims, they 
can still travel as an alliance until final registration.
9. PLAYER RULES, ADVANCEMENT AND 2024 CONVERSION
A practical level-12 party setup
All new pregens begin with one short-term objective, one faction they understand, and one doubt about Hell’s 
bureaucracy. The DM should hand them the nine Charter articles before any signature. Players may review, reject, 
propose amendments, seek a proxy or sign as a joint slate. A character who refuses can participate in an exploratory 
capacity until choosing a claim, without acquiring contestant Crown Marks.
Crown Marks are public rewards for genuine Proofs, not transferable currencies merely because someone writes a 
counterfeit receipt. A joint slate can pool eligible Proofs when it is registered. The party should keep a shared page 

with: current claim type, each Proof, additional Marks, outstanding agreements, current witness names, and the 
proposed order of succession.
2024 conversion
The adventure’s maths assumes 2014-era class progression. For 2024-style play, use the newer base classes, 
proficiency progression and current spell text where it differs. Ruby Court patron starts at Warlock 3 rather than 
Warlock 1; its later features are at 6/10/14. Circle of Ashen Covenants starts at Druid 3 rather than Druid 2; later 
features are at 6/10/14. Path of the Blood-Price remains at Barbarian 3/6/10/14. Do not grant the same initial features 
twice when converting an existing character. For the three imported Scriptorium subclasses, follow that book’s 
conversion sidebar and the table’s declared class-feature timing.
A 2024 spell may have altered balance, duration or concentration. Use the modern spell’s full text for that base spell. 
The 17 new spells in Section 5 are explicitly homebrew and have the effects printed here. All use the character’s 
normal spellcasting modifier; an item states its own DC when it supplies a fixed one.
Fighting a legendary hag at level 17
The Heartstone arc is optional and advertised as extreme. The Queen is a mythic CR 30 antagonist, not a fair 
level-17 encounter in isolation. Strengthening a party with items, legal injunctions, the testimony of the Three 
Witnesses, and high-level spells may give them a slim, meaningful chance. Players should not expect ordinary 
attrition and damage alone to carry them to victory. No part of the Charter requires Misty’s death to win the throne. 
Misty can be bargained with, outmanoeuvred, sponsored, publicly defeated in law, or physically challenged at 
enormous risk.
Character journals
At the end of each session, note: (1) one promise you fulfilled; (2) one promise you are considering; (3) one 
person who now has cause to trust or fear you. These are roleplay records, not soul penalties. At levels 13, 15 and 
17, revisit each character’s governance manifesto. Ask whether the ambition that brought them to Avernus is the 
same ambition they still hold.
Scriptorium compatibility reference
The Night of the Red Clause, Appendix G, The Asmodeun Scriptorium, contains 26 previous contractual spells, 
three subclasses (School of Infernal Contracts wizard, Oath of the Infernal Lord paladin, Contract Domain cleric), 
seven formal scroll contracts, and the Ledger subsystem with Draft DCs, Loophole Ratings and a soul-tithe track. 
This book adds three new subclasses, seventeen new spells, fourteen items, and eight complete Championship 
contract forms. It neither supersedes the original spell descriptions nor declares any signer to have accepted a 
contract they did not see. The two books can be used alongside the Red Clause Scriptorium as a single contract-magic 
rules family.
