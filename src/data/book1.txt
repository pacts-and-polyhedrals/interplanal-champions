1. RUNNING THE CHAMPIONSHIP
The five promises of this adventure
1. The throne is real. A victor may lawfully become Lord, Lady or Council of the First. Asmodeus retains his sovereignty over 
Hell, but the prize isn’t withdrawn through a last-minute gotcha.

2. The contract is knowable. Every contestant receives the complete Charter before signing. An ordinary failed die roll never 
transfers an unwilling soul.
3. The random tables have guardrails. They introduce volatility, bargains, hazards and foes appropriate for level 12. They do 
not unexpectedly drop Tiamat or an ancient dragon into combat.
4. No compulsory PvP. Personal claims and co-regency are equally viable. Player-versus-player combat occurs only when the 
group has opted in.
5. Misty stays Misty. The established CR 30 mythic Hell Hag Queen remains powerful throughout. At levels 12-15 she operates 
through counsel, covens, proxies and the Oathshard. At level 16 her real lair and Heartstone can be discovered. At level 17, a 
well-equipped and well-prepared party can choose a brutally dangerous true-form confrontation. Her death requires defeating 
both phases and destroying her Heartstone.
Tone
Begin with the bureaucratic black comedy of The Night of the Red Clause. The invitation is ostentatious, the 
reception desks are cruelly inefficient, and demons are asked to queue. In Greyhurst, humour drains into survivor 
guilt and the cost of souls. At the Citadel, the tone becomes political horror: allies may be purchased, testimony may 
be weaponised, and a spectator’s cheer may be a legally significant act of consent. The final arena is magnificent, 
lethal, and orderly in the most terrifying way.
Campaign roadmap and milestones
The championship is a campaign, not an overnight arena spectacle. The succession process requires proof of 
leadership, trials of strength and cunning, public claims, alliances, discovery of fraudulent entrants, and the final Rite 
of the Nine. A party may seek the throne jointly or split into rivals, subject to an agreed player-versus-player safety 
policy.

Lawful premise: what happened to Zariel?
In the usual Forgotten Realms continuity, Zariel rules Avernus and Bel remains an influential former ruler. This 
adventure makes one explicit homebrew divergence: Asmodeus has placed Zariel’s appointment under a conditional 
Succession Audit because the Blood War, infernal expenditure, and the failed Red Clause precedent have given him 
political reasons to reassess command. Zariel remains incumbent and has full rights to be represented by a proxy. She 
is not dead, secretly removed, or casually outmatched by level-12 heroes. Asmodeus has lawfully made the future 
office available to a challenge under his new Ninth Article. If your ongoing campaign has redeemed or removed 
Zariel, replace her proxy with Bel’s claimant and keep the same mechanical structure.

2. THE SUCCESSION AUDIT
What Asmodeus really wants
Asmodeus’s immediate goal is a new precedent: a throne may be won through an enforceable competition of 
Force, Cunning, and Legitimate Claim, rather than solely through appointment or military coup. He expects to gain 
a ruthless, competent First-Layer ruler; a public demonstration of infernal due process; and a way to keep ambitious 
archdevils fighting inside rules he authored. He will honour a lawful result because that result is more useful to him 
than a fraudulent contest.
An important tension: he values deviousness, but deviousness demonstrated by violating a Charter he himself signed 
is incompetence, not victory. Challengers who use the rules against one another impress him. Challengers who expose 
fraud may embarrass his staff and still earn his respect.

Claim types
Sole claim: One contestant is nominated for the throne; party allies can gain offices, rewards, or seats on a council 
without being bound as subjects.
Joint claim: Two or more contestants sign a Covenant of Joint Regency (Book II). They share the single executive 
office and its responsibilities. The contract has explicit decision and dissolution rules; it isn’t a secret trap.
Proxy claim: A contestant represents a patron or faction. The identity and authority of the patron must be disclosed 
to the Brass Arbiter before the final verdict.
Withdrawal: Anyone can leave the tournament before final investiture under the Charter’s notice provision. No 
automatic soul forfeiture occurs.

Primary adversary: Misty, Queen of Barbed Chains
Misty is not a disposable arena monster. She is the established Hell Hag Queen: one of the feared Daughters of the 
First Flame, patron of the Pyric Triune covens, and an expert in curse-weaving, soul custody, memory harvesting 
and infernal precedent. Her established true form is CR 30, AC 20, initial HP 244, Charisma 26, save DC 25; Hag 
Queen’s Deception restores 144 HP and moves the encounter to her resting place. A second defeat draws her back to 
her Heartstone; permanent victory requires destroying it. Preserve those elements for the level-17 optional 
confrontation rather than downscaling her.
In this adventure her aim is not simply to wear the crown. She wants a clause granting the hags perpetual first 
refusal on debts, memories and secrets collected by whichever ruler wins. She can sponsor her own champion if 
negotiations fail. Her case is disturbingly competent: unlike Nicholas Red Clause, Misty avoids obvious forged 
addresses or concealed service terms.
Her agents are Mother Sable (coven barrister), the Oathshard (a limited manifestation for levels 13-15), and an 
invisible Hag Eye that observes proceedings. The queen enters the story personally in Act IV, can be bargained with 
at level 16, and may be fought in her true form at level 17. Destroying an Oathshard has no effect on her own hit 
points or Heartstone.
Principal reveal
The inverted summoning circle in Greyhurst isn’t just summoning devils. It registers the town’s lost names as 
unclaimed contestants, permitting their identities and allegiance to be put into the tournament’s asset pool. Misty 
wants these names as collateral; Osric Kane wants military protection; Janella wants the circle undone so her family 
and her golems can be free. This makes the original mansion, golem and summoning scenes components of the 
succession plot.
The Championship Charter: DM quick law
Every contestant gets the full public Charter printed in Book II. These are its binding rules:
1. A contestant must give informed, voluntary assent. Signing is not the same as pledging a soul.
2. Each candidate must earn Survival, Deviousness and Dominion proofs. An approved slate may combine members’ proofs.
3. There is no mandatory death match. PvP is prohibited unless opponents separately agree to a duel under Article VII.

4. Legal challenges may be filed with the impartial Brass Arbiter; a documented challenge pauses the specific disputed process, 
not every event across Avernus.
5. Soul collateral requires a separate, conspicuous instrument. It cannot arise by inference from competition entry.
6. A co-regency is permitted if all co-rulers sign and publicly disclose their governance articles before the final bell.
7. The winner receives a real investiture, conditional obligations to defend the First Layer and obey Asmodeus’s overarching 
laws, and enforceable rights over its government.
Contract analysis procedure: keep the Red Clause system
Use the existing Asmodeun Scriptorium’s Ledger for long-term bargains: drafting scope sets Draft DC 
10/13/16/19/22 and initial Loophole Rating 3/4/5/6/8 respectively; successful drafting reduces loopholes, extra time 
and a contract devil improve quality, scrutiny identifies gaps, and a hearing exploits them. The championship adds 
Standing (who may file), Exhibits (why a claim is true), and Remedy (what the court can actually order).
Safety and compatibility ruling: The Championship Charter expressly waives the Scriptorium’s ordinary 5% 
soul court fee for all official championship petitions. Outside this Charter, treat any soul-tithe court fee as an 
explicit cost of an agreed infernal arbitration instrument, never as an automatic deduction from an unsigned 
character’s soul for failing a skill check. This narrow tournament interpretation prevents involuntary soul forfeiture 
and preserves the challenge’s meaningful legal risks. Spell effects can delay collection; they do not rewrite clear 
signed terms by fiat.
Tribunal challenge: three stages
A character uses an action during time-sensitive scenes, or an exchange during social scenes, to State Standing, 
Tender Evidence, Request Remedy. Clear documentary proof of an objective violation automatically establishes it. 
Otherwise, let the player argue a real interpretation and make an appropriate Intelligence (Investigation or Religion), 
Wisdom (Insight), or Charisma (Persuasion or Deception) check against DC 15 (routine), 18 (disputed), or 21 (high-
court precedent). Deception may win an improvised procedural gambit but may expose the claimant to sanctions if 
later proven false. One petition per side per round prevents high-Charisma spam.
Winning an objection can: remove a hostile contract effect, delay one arena tick, neutralise a hostile exhibit, or win a 
Crown Mark. No single success both wins the championship and disposes of an unrelated adversary’s rightful claim.
3. CHAPTER I — DEAD ALREADY
Original maps: Map 1, Welcome to Hell; Map 2, Unexpected Findings.
Target runtime: 2–3 hours in the full adventure, 35 minutes in the tournament cut.
Primary goal: Obtain Proof of Survival and the first indications that identities can be taken from people who are not 
dead.
Map 1: Welcome to Hell
The original adventure opens with the characters’ souls ripped from limbo by Asmodeus and dumped into Avernus, 
their equipment stripped. To accommodate returning Red Clause survivors, this version gives the party a choice: enter 
alive through the appointed summoning gate (retaining gear), or sign a controlled Death-in-Absence Writ, 
temporarily separating body and soul for a greater opening reward. Do not force the second option on players. A 
character who chooses the latter returns to their own body after the entrance trial, and receives a one-use Brass 
Reprieve token to reroll a failed death save during this adventure.

Read aloud
Darkness with the weight of a grave presses against you. You smell hot brass. A thousand signatures 
scratch across paper somewhere just beyond your hearing.
Then red sand pours from a sky with no sun. A broken bridge spans a river glowing with molten light. On 
the far bank, a sign leans sideways: WELCOME, PROSPECTIVE SOVEREIGN. ALL 
COMPLAINTS MUST BE FILED IN TRIPLICATE.
A voice speaks from the clouds, smooth and amused.
“If you wish to rule Hell, do show me you can survive the reception.”
Keyed encounter locations
1A, the Counting Gate. Two imps ask contestants to declare their names, chosen slates, and whether they claim the 
right of appeal. The answer yes is free and lawful. The imps pretend that it requires a filing surcharge. A successful 
DC 15 Insight or evidence-based legal argument earns a tiny brass receipt marked EXEMPT FROM FRAUDULENT 
FEES, an exhibit usable once later.
1B, the Ash Bridge. The crossing is about 60 feet long, with broken platforms over burning flow. Use a three-
success-before-two-failures group challenge (DC 16). Dexterity (Acrobatics), Strength (Athletics), vehicle 
proficiency, magical traversal and clever support can all contribute. Failed challenges inflict 3d8 fire damage to one 
exposed character, or consume a resource to prevent it; avoid instant death from lava at a procedural entry point.
1C, the Three Witnesses. Three hovering skull-like notaries can confirm that the party crossed without sacrificing an 
unwilling bystander. Obtaining their testimony creates the Proof of Survival. If the party rescued a stranded courier, 
it also gains one bonus Survival Crown Mark.
1D, the Damaged War Machine. This construct is not automatically hostile. It carries a shattered metal tag bearing 
maker initials J.G. and a missing socket for infernal-glass. Investigation DC 15 reveals it is a smaller golem from the 
same design family as the dormant alley construct in Greyhurst. This is the first breadcrumb for Janella Greenbottle.
Map 1 roll points
At each of the original map’s numbered encounter markers, roll once on the appropriate d100 table in Appendix A. 
Do not roll repeatedly if characters circle the same spot. A treasure marker grants one reward from the proportional 
loot table; no unattended artifact or ancient-dragon encounter appears here.
Map 2: Unexpected Findings
The rocky banks of a lava-cut channel lead towards the ruins of Greyhurst and, eventually, the Brass Citadel. A black 
branch of the river Styx touches the ashflow in one location; drinking it or deliberately immersing oneself risks 
memory loss, but the red lava should not be misrepresented as the Styx itself.
The warning written in iron
THE DEAD ARE ELIGIBLE. THE LIVING ARE EXPENDABLE. THE UNCLAIMED ARE 
PROPERTY OF THE HOST.
Beneath the inscription someone has carved: THERE IS NO SUCH THING AS UNCLAIMED.
Scene 2A: the floating docket
A sealed brass casket drifts along the current. Recovering it requires a clever crossing, telekinesis, or DC 17 Athletics 
with proper precautions. Inside is an obsolete tournament bracket listing contestants who have been dead for decades 

but have somehow been entered this year. One name is a member of Janella’s family. This is Exhibit A: The False 
Entrants.
Scene 2B: the bargain of safe passage
A ferryman offers passage for “the one memory you can most easily afford to lose.” The exact memory and its 
consequence must be named before signing; he cannot take an unspecified memory by trick. Characters may pay 
coin, build a crossing, fly, or bargain for a defined memory instead. If they do make a bargain, use the Book II 
Memory Escrow template. A character who pays a memory has it restored if the contract’s stated return condition is 
met; this is not a permanent trap without recourse.
Scene 2C: a contestant under attack
A rival band, the Ashclad Petitioners, is being pursued by one Ash-Scrap Golem and two lesser scavengers. The party 
can aid them, watch, or negotiate. Saving a rival earns a future witness and an optional Dominion lead. Taking them 
prisoner is permitted by the Charter only if they actually consent to a declared duel, or if they are incapacitated 
lawfully during a hazard.
The Ashclad petitioners
Their leader Vexa Corven, a sarcastic human duelist, is strong enough to survive but too politically naive to contest 
Misty’s documents. She can become ally, rival, or sworn witness. Her declared personal claim is genuine. Treat her as 
a living person with ambitions, not as guaranteed cannon fodder.
Transition to Greyhurst
As the party leaves Map 2, show the ruins of a village under an inverted red summoning circle and a field of 
motionless metal figures. A notary announces that Greyhurst’s dead have “kindly volunteered as contestants.” That is 
the first fully serious horror reveal.
4. CHAPTER II — THE FALLEN
Original maps: Map 3, The Fallen; Map 4, Sorcerous Implications; Map 5, Sorcerous Implications 2.
Target runtime: 3–5 hours.
Primary goal: Uncover the town’s stolen names, decide the golemancer’s fate, obtain Proof of Deviousness and a 
path to Dominion.
What happened to Greyhurst
Mayor Osric Kane, called the Lemure King by horrified survivors, attempted to summon a devil army to protect 
Greyhurst. He inverted the inscription on the town’s summoning circle. Instead of calling devils in, the mechanism 
registered the villagers’ living identities and cast their souls into the contract machinery of Avernus. Many returned as 
lemures, others as disoriented fragments, and some became paper-only contestants. Misty’s agents supplied the 
missing legal phrases and now own the copied registry unless the characters intervene.
The golemancer, Janella Greenbottle, is a halfling artificer who built defensive constructs for Greyhurst. Her family 
includes Marus and Lila. Her most ambitious creation, the Iron Warder Prototype, lies inert in an alley because its 
infernal-glass control core was removed by Janella herself when she learned that Kane had overlaid dangerous 
infernal orders on its protective mandate. Kane subsequently stole the stored core and sold access to its command 
record to Misty’s agents. The core stores original commands and evidence that Kane knew the ritual’s true purpose. 

Janella can be ally, reluctant rival, or antagonist depending on how the party treats her and her family. Her creations 
are not interchangeable loot.
Map 3: The Fallen
3A, the Drowned Tavern. A table is set for a celebration that never happened. The guest list has become a 
tournament roster. The last three names have the same handwriting as the current succession registrar. Show the 
missing name of Marus prominently. Investigation DC 15 provides a duplicate of Exhibit A if needed.
3B, the Alley Golem. A weathered iron giant slumps beside a wall, its chest plate open around a missing core. Its 
inscription reads JAN ELLA / WARD HOUSE, NEVER WAR HOUSE. A successful DC 15 Arcana or Investigation 
check shows the core slot and the defensive command language. Do not run a normal Iron Golem stat block as a 
surprise fight. This is the damaged Iron Warder Prototype in the bestiary: a CR 7 chassis until restored, with an 
explicit initiative routine and no fire-healing loop.
3C, the Greenbottle Family. Janella and Lila hide in a partially collapsed house. Marus was taken to the mansion 
after discovering Kane had stolen the core Janella removed and had arranged to sell its recorded commands to 
Misty’s agents. The family may initially fear the party. Calm explanation, proof from the tavern, or a good act toward 
Lila opens negotiation without a check. If Janella thinks the party means to steal her work, she activates one Ash-
Scrap Golem as a deterrent and attempts escape rather than fighting to the death.
3D, the False Mayor’s Guard. Two armoured cadaver collectors and a brass registrar march with warrants naming 
souls as “unclaimed.” The characters can destroy them or challenge the false basis of the warrants using Exhibit A. A 
documented, valid challenge forces the registrar to suspend collection and creates Exhibit B: The False Service 
Order.
3E, the Evacuation Bell. Ringing an old village bell alerts all surviving families, but also triggers patrols. It is a 
choice about whom to protect. If the party openly organizes the survivors into a civic defence committee, its members 
can swear voluntary allegiance; that is a path toward Proof of Dominion, independent of Janella’s help.
Missing core: three recoverable clues
1. Janella’s forge ledger identifies her controller as a removable infernal-glass heart with maker initials J.G.
2. An inverted rune found beneath the mayor’s study table matches the core’s command socket.
3. Misty’s counsellor has prepared a custody deed offering to return the core in exchange for “the first authority exercised by the 
future regent.” The players can reject this plainly disclosed bargain.
Map 4: Sorcerous Implications — the mansion below
This is Kane’s former seat of government. Its original traps, infernal script and summoning puzzle become elements 
of an evidence-gathering sequence rather than unrelated dangers.
Scene 4A: the house of witnesses
A spectral mayoral clerk will testify only if the party submits an actual question and discloses its intended use. It is 
frightened of Misty, but truthful about the terms it signed. Offer a meaningful choice: promise to protect its name in 
exchange for testimony, or command it through magic and risk the evidence being disputed as coerced.
Scene 4B: the inverse summoning circle
Four plinths bear inscriptions corresponding to Name, Consent, Witness, Jurisdiction. Their correct sequence is the 
lawful structure of a summoning contract: Name → Consent → Witness → Jurisdiction. Investigating the mayor’s 

notes, seeing through illusion, or having expertise in the Red Clause’s Ledger reveals the sequence. If a player states 
and demonstrates the logic, no roll is required.
Incorrect combinations do not immediately unleash an army. They trigger one hazard from the Mansion 
Consequences table and reset the mechanism. After two mistakes, the diagram visibly reveals which one term was 
misplaced, so the challenge never becomes a blind guessing trap.
Activating the circle correctly releases a record of the failed ritual, reveals the altered contestant registry, and awards 
Proof of Deviousness. It also produces Exhibit C: the unmodified command transcript. The transcript shows Kane 
deliberately concealing the soul transfer from villagers.
Scene 4C: Misty’s first offer
Mother Sable, a coven barrister wearing a velvet lawyer’s hood, arrives under a declared truce. She offers to return 
the golem core and corroborate the mayor’s treason if the party signs Misty’s Memorandum of Memory Escrow. She 
gives the full document and answers direct questions about obligations. Misty needs a ruler she can influence, not 
another fraudulent Red Clause.
If the party refuses, Mother Sable is courteous and tries a lawful counteroffer. If they attack her during the truce, the 
Brass Arbiter can record the breach as a procedural disadvantage, but never steals an unsigned soul. If they prove that 
Mother Sable has concealed a material annex, a DC 18 court challenge can strike it and force the contract to be 
redrafted.
Scene 4D: the soul-split mirrors
The original mansion’s mirror-trap concept becomes a three-turn challenge. A mirror shows each character a version 
of themselves enthroned on Avernus. The reflection offers a specific exploit, such as gaining one Crown Mark by 
surrendering a genuine ally’s trust. A character can reject the offer without penalty; accepting requires a written or 
spoken bargain. Using logic, Insight DC 17, or shattering the correct sigil allows the party to exit. Any mirror creature 
that emerges uses the rival duelist statistics rather than a free additional high-CR demon.
Map 5: Sorcerous Implications II — the mayor’s upper floor
5A, the Treasury of Names. The party can locate Marus, or his remaining identity if already transformed. Freeing 
him means defeating a custody lock, not necessarily a monster. Use DC 17 Investigation/Thieves’ Tools/Arcana or a 
fight with one Cadaver Collector. The lock is a contractual seal, so a successful argument that Marus never agreed to 
be registered also opens it.
5B, Kane’s office. Osric Kane (bestairy) attempts to justify his actions: he believed turning the town into infernal 
assets would earn permanent military protection. A candid, convincing confrontation can force him to confess; 
violence can subdue him; the record offers proof regardless. If the players allow Kane to offer restitution, his 
testimony may help rescue villagers, but it does not absolve him.
5C, the infernal-glass core. The stolen heart lies behind a warded wall. Inserted into the Iron Warder, it wakes the 
construct with its original defensive orders and a perfect recording of Kane’s sale. If players instead give the core to 
Misty or Bel, they receive a different faction promise and may make Janella an enemy.
5D, the warden’s choice. Janella asks the party to recognise the Iron Warder as a guardian with limited autonomy, 
not a commodity to be sold by the next ruler. Granting that right creates a favourable Dominion witness and 
establishes that the party can govern an asset without enslaving it. Refusing to help, stealing her work or coercing her 
can still produce forceful Dominion under the Charter, but makes the later politics more dangerous.

Proof of Dominion
Award the Proof when the party either obtains the voluntary sworn support of a meaningful Greyhurst group, secures 
Janella’s cooperation and the Warder’s protective mandate, or wins a legitimate military victory while defending the 
town. Do not require Janella to submit personally: protect her autonomy to preserve the story’s moral tension.
Misty’s second legal maneuver
Once the party has at least two Proofs, Misty files Petition M-9, claiming any crown candidate using “borrowed 
memory, replicated identity, or construct testimony” owes her guild a perpetual evidence royalty. This is weak on its 
own, but she has a stamped annex showing consent from Osric Kane—who had no authority to contract on behalf of 
the villagers.
Present the annex in full. A character can defeat the claim by proving (i) Kane lacked authority over individual 
names, (ii) the transferred memories were not his to sell, or (iii) the annex misidentifies the contracting party. Each 
separate, persuasive argument plus appropriate evidence yields a success in a two successes before two failures 
hearing, DC 18 when disputed. A correct document establishing lack of authority counts as one automatic success. 
Success grants a legal Stay of Hag Lien for the final arena and deprives Misty of one interference token. Failure does 
not end the adventure; Misty gains one interference token and offers a lawful settlement.
Transition to the War of Claims
After Greyhurst, the route opens toward a fortress of bronze and brass whose walls are made from ancient war 
engines. The party sees banners of Zariel, Bel and numerous competitors. A great amphitheatre erupts with the sound 
of combat, but the final rite is many weeks away. Claimants must survive the war of sponsorship, public trials and an 
increasingly lethal series of preliminary arenas before the Seat of Avernus can be decided. The announcement begins: 
ALL CLAIMANTS TO THE FIRST SHALL PRESENT THEMSELVES FOR INSPECTION.
5. CHAPTER III - THE WAR OF CLAIMS
Levels: 14 to 15. Sessions: 8-11. Place: The roads and outposts between Greyhurst and the Brass Citadel. Question: 
Can the candidates gather consent, alliances and a reputation for competent rule without selling their independence?
The three agendas
• Asmodeus’s agenda: The First Layer needs a ruler who can hold ground in the Blood War, keep the soul economy functional, 
and survive rivals. The tournament converts coup and patronage into repeatable governance selection. This is not a promise of 
independence from Nessus: a First-Layer ruler remains subordinate to Asmodeus.
• Misty’s agenda: A legally recognised monopoly on memories, curses and disputed identities in the winner’s realm. She prefers 
victory through a perpetual service concession, but may seek the throne through a controlled candidate.
• The hidden registrar’s agenda: Deputy Registrar Hesper Valrick survived the collapse of the Red Clause’s fiscal machinery. 
Hesper has seeded the tournament with paper champions, stolen identities whose Crown Marks can be harvested and 
transferred. Misty supplied the template but does not control every use of it. Exposing Hesper is the campaign’s evidence trail; 
determining whether Misty can be held responsible is a later question of principal and agent.
A clock that travels with the campaign
Track three public clocks from zero to six; advance them at chapter conclusions and for conspicuous choices. Blood 
War Pressure rises when supply lines collapse; Hag Lien rises when mortals accept broad memory/identity 
concessions; Public Legitimacy rises when claimants keep promises, protect bystanders, and win clean hearings. 
Clocks never erase completed adventures, but alter who shows up in the finals.

Session 8: The market of claimed names
Scene: A travelling bazaar inside the ribcage of an ancient infernal siege beast. Every stall sells a legal abstraction: a 
day of bravery, the right to disagree, the smell of one’s home. Announce that a champion named Sergeant Orro 
offers to sell the party a regiment of names for thirty soul-coins. They are the names of Greyhurst villagers.
Encounter objective: Recover the roster without ratifying trafficking in non-consenting persons. Three approaches: 
prove Orro cannot transfer what he never owned (Exhibits A-C, no roll); negotiate a defined custody-of-evidence loan 
(DC 17 Persuasion with an explicit exit clause); raid the ledger stall (2 Ash-Scrap Golems, 1 Writ Bailiff, civilian 
complications). Orro flees if his legal authority is disproven.
Key clue: Each false entrant bears the red serialization H-25/RETURN, linking the tournament’s fraudulent registry 
to the Red Clause case. This is Exhibit E: Chain of Title.
Choice: Free names immediately, forfeiting their value as evidence until they are recorded by a neutral witness; or 
copy and notarise first at the expense of time. Both work if players propose a sensible method to protect authenticity.
Session 9: Trial of the Ash March (Trial IV, Dominion)
A convoy carrying food, gears, wounded devils and displaced mortals must reach an outpost before a demon skirmish 
force. Three factions offer help: Bel’s engineers, Zariel’s cavalry, and Greyhurst’s reclaimed golems. Each asks for a 
published, negotiable concession, not a secret soul tax.
Mass-battle procedure (no army simulator required): Run three exchanges. Each exchange requires a group plan, 
then two relevant checks against DC 17. Success scores an Advantage; failure scores a Cost such as lost cargo, 
casualties, or an exhausted ally. The same character cannot make both checks in one exchange. Spells and creative 
engineering can substitute for checks if they plausibly solve the problem. At two Advantages, convoy survives; at 
three, survivors publicly support the claimants. At two Costs, run a short fight (2 Horned Raiders represented by Writ 
Bailiffs with wings, 1 Ash-Scrap Golem). At three Costs, the outpost survives but loses half its supplies and Blood 
War Pressure rises.
Proving Dominion: The claimants must issue one clear, public order and accept responsibility for its consequences. 
A successful evacuation, disciplined defence or negotiated alliance qualifies. Do not require all PCs to agree who 
commands; joint command can be binding under their slate agreement.

Session 10: The Whispering Docket (Trial V, Scales)
In a courthouse attached to a moving fortress, Misty’s counsel petitions for the First Right of Collection: the right to 
seize memories from any future Avernus debtor before other creditors. Hesper offers to testify against her in 
exchange for immunity.
Court facts: Hesper can prove Misty’s lawyers drafted the identity-transfer template. Misty can prove she never 
signed the particular forged Greyhurst assignments. Both are true. The party must distinguish authoring a dangerous 
form from authorising this instance of fraud.
Three stages: Admissibility, authority, and remedy. In each stage, one PC presents the governing evidence and 
another may act as witness. Correct evidence wins a stage without a check; an improvised case is DC 18 
Investigation/Religion/Persuasion. Two winning stages deny universal first-refusal. Three also impose a two-session 
injunction preventing the coven from collecting contestant memories. Zero or one permits a limited thirty-day 
concession unless the party withdraws its claim before the judgment is final.
Hesper’s lie: Hesper is not merely a witness. His immunity document attempts to cover future fraud. The words 
including acts yet to be discovered are visible if anyone reads the document. Characters who strike that 
phrase can still take his testimony.
Session 11: The Trial of False Faces (Trial VI, Deception)
Three masked champions enter a labyrinthine legal masquerade. One is a living contestant, one is a construct 
replicating a living person, and one is a memory stitched together from a deceased volunteer. No choice should 
depend on identifying a creature’s type as inherently illegitimate: the relevant question is who genuinely consented to 
be represented.
Run four chambers in sequence: Name (a written roster), Witness (two competing accounts), Consideration (who 
received the benefit), and Remedy (who would suffer from enforcement). A group that follows the paper trail finds 
the real fraud: the living claimant is being impersonated in writing by a validly constructed proxy beyond the scope of 
its mandate. The party can demand a new signature, obtain witness protection, or expose Hesper. Each success earns 
one legitimacy point; three wins a public commendation and level 15. Fewer gives the players a partial success but 
advances Hag Lien by one.

6. CHAPTER IV - BLOOD AND BRASS
Levels: 15 to 16. Sessions: 12-15. Place: Brass Citadel and outer arena complex. Question: Can a claimant survive 
the political and military machinery required to hold the throne?
The Citadel
Use the original adventure’s brass-coliseum artwork and arena plan as the central visual. Expand its surrounding 
district into five keyed locations: Hall of Registrants (public brackets and legal standing); The Iron Quarter 
(construct yards and Janella’s workshop); Gallery of Unclaimed Kings (defeated claimants’ names); The Pyric 
Embassy (Misty’s representation); Asmodeus’s Balcony (sealed until the final rite). The Bronze/Brass names in the 
old module can be treated as local vernacular for the same fortification.
Session 12: Arrival and registration
The official claims desk tries to register every member of the party as a separate contestant, even if they want shared 
rule. The published Charter explicitly permits joint slates. Hesper has issued an unlawful circular interpreting 
claimant as singular.
Give players the circular. If they cite the original Charter, the clerk must register a joint slate. A DC 18 Insight check 
reveals why Hesper opposes it: joint claims reduce the number of isolated souls that can be bought, blackmailed or 
harvested. If they choose separate claims, document consent and establish a no-PvP contingency using the League of 
Claimants contract.
Encounter: A confident rival offers a formal contest on the practice floor. This is a nonlethal 3-round showcase. 
Champions score points for controlling the centre, protecting an assigned witness, and completing a declared aim, not 
simply damage. Reward tactical variety. Avoid a full to-the-death set piece immediately after travel.
Session 13: The Golemancer’s War (Trial VII, Siege)
Hesper’s agents steal Janella’s master command seal and attempt to turn her warders against the Citadel. The party 
must choose between defending the district, exposing the stolen-order chain, and capturing the architect of the attack.
Three simultaneous objectives (4 combat rounds): Defend the workshop civilians; disable two command pylons 
(AC 16, 30 HP or DC 17 Arcana/Thieves’ Tools); and preserve Hesper’s authorization parchment. A PC can use an 
action to accomplish one discrete objective; an allied autonomous warder contributes once per round rather than 
taking a full separate turn.
At default difficulty, deploy one Iron Warder Prototype (damaged), two Ash-Scrap Golems, and two Writ 
Bailiffs split across entry corridors. If players restored Janella’s core and gained her trust, the Warder instead fights 

alongside them and replace it with a Contract Sentinel. For four characters, remove one bailiff; for six, add one 
bailiff after round 2.
Janella’s dilemma: She can broadcast a universal kill command to shut the constructs down, or upload their 
recovered personalities and relinquish exclusive control. The former is tactically easier; the latter supports her long-
term goal of construct personhood. The party’s choice affects whether the warders support their final claim without 
being contractually enslaved.
Session 14: The Preliminary Pits (Trial VIII, Resolve)
The first public arena is a showcase, not the entire championship. Roll once on the tempered Arena Table for each of 
three distinct rounds; each has one objective: survive, control, or persuade a hostile witness. Allow applicants to 
earn victory through evidence and diplomacy even when beasts spawn.
Round 1, Ashfall: Defeat a chosen rival team or hold the two control braziers for three rounds. Environmental d6 at 
initiative count 20, DC 17.
Round 2, The Claimant’s Price: A sealed gate opens only if competitors choose who receives the prize. Three 
paths: share the prize and fight a stronger guard, award it to one person and deal with political resentment, or identify 
that the gate’s winner clause includes a joint legal entity under the charter.
Round 3, The Enemy’s Witness: A prosecution witness offers damning evidence against the party. The witness is 
being legally compelled to reveal the truth but may be quoting a forged document. Let the party impeach the source 
rather than silencing the witness. If they protect the witness and expose the forgery, gain two Crown Marks and 
Public Legitimacy +1.
Session 15: The coup that cannot be filed
Hesper attempts to transfer accumulated Crown Marks into one synthetic contender, the Vacant King, a name given 
a body of notarised brass. The experiment is not fully Misty’s doing; it threatens her interests too. She offers a 
temporary legal ceasefire to defeat Hesper. Bel offers siege engines; Zariel’s representative offers soldiers. Each asks 
different concessions.
Boss scene: Hesper directs the Vacant King while a championship stamp prints counterfeit warrants. Destroying the 
stamp (AC 17, 40 HP) removes the King’s regeneration; proving the entrants were never validly registered also 
suppresses the effect. Run the Vacant King (below) with one Writ Bailiff, no large additional waves. When defeated, 
Hesper attempts to escape via an administrative elevator carrying the true roster. Pursuit or public seizure leads to a 
tribunal and level 16.
Reveal: Hesper was specifically testing whether an office rather than an individual could inherit a soul, opening an 
avenue for corporate ownership of rulers. Misty despises this precedent because it bypasses her personal bargaining 
model. Asmodeus permits the investigation, but does not intervene to protect Hesper.
7. CHAPTER V - THE HAG QUEEN’S DUE
Levels: 16-17. Sessions: 16-19. Place: The Pyric Triune, Misty’s domain and resting place. Question: What price 
will the heroes pay to reach a victory that cannot be appealed?
Misty is a campaign-scale political adversary, not a mid-level miniboss. Her actual form is the pre-existing CR 30 
Hell Hag Queen. At this level the party has enough resources to attempt an extraordinary battle, but without 
preparation her DC 25 magic, action economy and mythic phase are likely to overwhelm even well-equipped 

characters. The campaign should make that danger legible. The Heartstone is not a surprise immunity discovered 
after victory: rumours, documents and a direct warning establish its function beforehand.
Three ways through this chapter
Alliance: Sign a narrowly defined concession that gives the Pyric Triune an advisory office, explicitly excluding 
involuntary soul collection and property in citizens’ memories. Misty honours a properly sealed pact if it gives her 
real political influence. Contestants retain a route to the throne; later governance faces factional consequences.
Legal defeat: Prove that Misty’s claim to the stolen Greyhurst names, false entrants or golem memories exceeds her 
chain of title. A ruling can force her to surrender Hesper’s stolen dossiers and renounce collection priority without 
destroying her. It wins a significant final-arena advantage.
Personal confrontation: At level 17, locate her Heartstone, secure a means to break it, enter her resting place and 
fight Misty in both mythic phases. This is a real optional battle, not a projection. Winning gives the party the Pyric 
Records, the Chainbreaker Seal and the possibility of ending her reign as Hag Queen.
Session 16: The Queens’ Due
Misty grants the party an audience in a chamber of hanging iron roses. Every guest sees a memory they never 
willingly told her, but she does not claim a right to own it merely by seeing it. She is personable, amused by 
cleverness and utterly ruthless about payment.
‘The Red Clause was a fool, my sweets. He tried to take what no one gave him. I prefer to make people 
beg me to take it. Now tell me: which of you intends to wear the First Crown, and how much of the 
kingdom are you prepared to sell?’
Social encounter: Misty proposes the Perpetual First Refusal Writ (Book II). Provide the full text. She does not 
conceal an appendix. Her trap is economic: even limited percentages of future memory disputes will give her 
influence. The party can negotiate a sunset clause, independent audit, prohibition on children’s memories, or personal 
liability borne by the ruler instead of citizens. Each material concession requires something meaningful in return; do 
not let a single DC 20 Persuasion check erase the contract.
Evidence: A Hag Eye reports her full true name is not recoverable from public registries. Three separate clues 
identify the Heartstone’s location, provided the party undertakes the hunt. If they reject Misty’s offer, she still invites 
them to try their case; she enjoys an opponent who knows the price.
Session 17: The Three Witnesses of the Heartstone
The Heartstone lies in the Thorn Reliquary, reachable only through three independent seals. The party can acquire 
each seal in any order.
1. The Witness of Origin: An ancient hag remembers Misty’s first binding and knows the Heartstone must remain within her 
resting place during mythic transformation. Bargain with her for a memory she already possesses, or protect her from Misty’s 
enforcers. She provides the Reliquary’s location and a drawing of the stone.
2. The Witness of Ownership: The estate register records who holds title to the Heartstone. It is held in trust by the Pyric Triune, 
not Misty as a person. A successful legal action can suspend the trust’s right to prevent entry. This does not destroy the stone.
3. The Witness of Death: A cracked hag-eye contains a recording: At first death the Queen returns to her bed; at second death 
she returns to the Heartstone. Study it to reveal the two-stage defeat condition before initiative is rolled.

Run one complex obstacle for each chosen witness: social bargaining, DC 18-21 investigation or Arcana, or a 
medium combat with two Writ Bailiffs and one Night Hag Advocate. No clue should be permanently inaccessible due 
to a single failed roll.
Session 18: Raid on the Thorn Reliquary
The party has three objectives. Disable the Soul Siphon (DC 19 Arcana or AC 18, 45 HP), establish legal right of 
entry (court injunction or signed search warrant), and reach the Heartstone antechamber (solve the 
Name/Consent/Witness/Jurisdiction seal). A raid without a warrant can still work physically, but incurs a final-
championship complaint from Misty’s surviving agents.
Battlefield concept: 90 x 90 feet, central sleeping alcove, two high bridges, four hanging iron roses that block line of 
sight, a Heartstone pedestal behind a prismatic screen. A trapped soul mosaic near the entrance can be freed and used 
to block one lair action per battle. Heartstone ward: AC 20, 70 HP, damage threshold 10, immune poison/psychic; 
not attackable until two of the three witness-seals have been suppressed. The screen can also be bypassed by an 
independent court order. Destroying it while Misty still lives does not automatically kill her, but prevents a retreat 
into it after her second defeat.
Run one tense guardian encounter here if needed (Night Hag Advocate + 2 Writ Bailiffs), then let the party short rest 
if they negotiate safe passage or secure the area. Do not stack this attrition and the true CR 30 fight without signalling 
what the choice means.
Session 19: Misty Unbound (optional level-17 mythic battle)
Read aloud:
Her crown is made of thorns that move when she does not. The Queen looks beyond your weapons to the 
seals in your hands. Then she smiles, not because she believes you harmless, but because you have finally 
become interesting. ‘The last adventurers who reached my bedchamber wished they had chosen the 
contract. Tell me - do you?’
Preserve the existing Misty: CR 30, AC 20, 244 HP, Charisma 26 and spell/ability save DC 25. Maintain Children 
of Hell, Soul Drain, Hell Hag’s Spines, Hag Eye, Nightmare Haunting, Etherealness and her actual Heartstone 
mechanics as already established. If using her prior complete stat block, use its exact action text rather than stacking a 
new version. The notes below are an encounter framework, not an assertion that an incomplete reconstruction is the 
original block.
Phase 1: The Queen fights in her audience chamber. She prefers control, one frightening focus target, and 
temptations that can be rejected. At 0 HP, Hag Queen’s Deception triggers: she teleports herself and the adventurers 
to her resting place, restores 144 HP, and immediately uses Children of Hell. Describe this as a scripted mythic 
phase, not a new creature entering battle.
Phase 2: The resting chamber is stranger and more personal. At initiative count 20, choose or roll one effect: chains 
(DC 19 Dex or restrained until next turn), memory storm (DC 19 Wis or 3d8 psychic and cannot take reactions until 
next turn), or heartstone pulse (Misty regains 10 HP unless ward has been suppressed). These are optional arena 
effects: if her established stat block already has potent lair actions, do not add them all.
Second defeat: If reduced to 0 HP again, Misty is drawn into her Heartstone. If it survives, she can return under her 
original rules. To end her permanently, characters must destroy the stone once accessible. If destroyed in advance, it 
cannot receive her; she perishes on the second defeat. Allow other creative or higher-level magic that explicitly 
defeats a soul-anchor to substitute if it satisfies the fiction and published rules.

Real odds and fair warnings
A CR 30 mythic enemy against four or five level-17 characters is extreme. Full rested resources, rare/very-rare items, 
allies or boons, Heartstone preparation and tactical competence might make victory possible, but it remains a slim 
chance, as desired. Do not present expected victory, quietly reduce her DC, or remove the mythic phase to equalise 
the fight. Equally, do not force the fight to gate the campaign’s conclusion. Offer three observable exits: a willing 
truce at the start, a route through the sealed doorway during combat, and a legally protected withdrawal if a claimant 
surrenders the current hearing without surrendering their soul.
Defeat consequences: The Pyric Triune splinters. The party may claim its evidence, but not automatically all 
members’ allegiance. The ruler who killed a queen must now govern the power vacuum. Asmodeus grants one bonus 
proof of Deviousness but not an automatic throne.
Survival consequences: Misty remains an independent power. If the party defeated her in law instead, she may 
grudgingly support a charter recognising limits on involuntary debt collection. If they sold her a concession, she 
becomes their most dangerous early minister.
8. CHAPTER VI - THE RITE OF THE NINE
Level: 17. Sessions: 20-24. Place: The Arena of Asmodeus at the Brass Citadel. Question: What kind of sovereign 
will the party become?
Conditions for entry
Each slate must present three Crown Proofs (Survival, Deviousness, Dominion), declaration of beneficiaries, a 
declared claim type, and notice of any debts that would materially compromise government. A party need not hold 
every bonus Mark. Denial of entry must cite a particular clause; if a registrar invents a rule, the Brass Arbiter must 
hear the challenge.
The Rite of the Nine uses nine moments of judgment across the campaign rather than requiring nine simultaneous 
boss fights: Claim (registration), Ember (arrival), Witness (Greyhurst), Dominion (convoy), Scales (court), Deception 
(masks), Siege (golems), Resolve (preliminaries), and Crown (final). The last rite tests the history of all eight others.
Session 20: Opening ceremony
Read aloud:
The arena rises from Avernus like a crown driven upside down into the earth. Fire circles the stands. A 
thousand devils roar for champions whose names they have purchased, cursed or memorised. Nine 
standards unfurl in black flame. A voice older than battle fills the amphitheatre: ‘No throne is beyond 
contest. No claim is beyond audit. Let those who would govern the First prove strength, cunning, and 
mastery of their own word.’
Asmodeus presides by an unassailable court-image; his presence is not a combat target. The incumbent interest 
(Zariel proxy, Bel proxy or setting-specific substitute) enters beside the party’s rivals. The party may submit joint or 
sole claims up until the opening judgment.
Sessions 21-22: Arena rounds
Arena template: Use original Map 6 as the lower fighting floor. Center platform counts as contested control; two 
circular summoning glyphs at the side can be suppressed; blue and yellow crystals produce hazards; four lava lines 

segment the floor; two chests are optional rewards. Use initiative count 20 for one environmental effect per round, 
not a full new hazard between every PC turn. Environmental die begins d6 with: 1 no effect, 2 electricity, 3 blue 
crystal, 4 lava, 5 yellow crystal, 6 summons.
Round A - Trial of Strength (4-6 rounds): Defeat the other slate or hold the centre uncontested for three rounds. 
The party faces Vexa and one sponsored champion, or the Vacant King’s replacement if Hesper survived. The crowd 
votes do not override actual results. Mercy towards a defeated opponent increases Public Legitimacy if consistent 
with a declared policy.
Round B - Trial of Deviousness (4 rounds): Four sealed orders fall into the arena. Each order offers a real tactical 
reward and a negotiable cost. Players can accept any, reject all, or challenge contradictions at a brass witness stand. 
One order is intentionally invalid because its purported signatory is a fake entrant; proving this disables an enemy 
ward. This prevents an all-combat party from being trapped while making the legal plot mechanically central.
Round C - Trial of Dominion (3-5 rounds): The arena transforms into a miniature besieged province. Three civilian 
teams and two rival troop groups appear as simulacra that still remember pain. Claimants must defend resources, 
declare priorities, and command. Score two points for preserving civilians, one for defeating the attacker, one for 
maintaining an agreement with a rival, one for a workable evacuation. Three points qualify. A contestant can win this 
trial without killing a single creature.
Session 23: The last appeal
Misty (or her successor), an incumbent proxy, and Hesper if alive each may lodge one last petition based on real acts 
of the campaign. The Brass Arbiter rules with published evidence, not hidden DM judgement. Run up to three 
petitions, each 10-15 minutes: one concerning consent, one concerning title, one concerning conflict of interest. Every 
petition states a disputed clause and the sought remedy. The party can submit counterevidence and cross-examine. No 
petition can independently confiscate a soul absent a valid prior signed contract.
A real final twist: Asmodeus offers a better contract than everyone expected: the throne, with actual power to 
rewrite Avernus’s civil procedures and appoint ministers, in return for enforceable military performance and 
recognition of his superior jurisdiction. It is not freedom from Hell; it is a genuine appointment with dreadful 
responsibilities. A player who refuses remains free to reject the crown.
Session 24: Crown, Council or Catastrophe
If one claimant has clearly won the trial, they may take the throne alone. If a joint slate wins, they may execute the 
public Covenant of Joint Regency (Book II), including voting, veto, offices and dissolution rules. If competing PCs 
are tied, let them negotiate a joint charter, appoint an independent steward, or agree to a consensual last duel. Never 
force PvP against the table’s preference.
Public judgment: Each player describes (1) one law they immediately enact, (2) one promise they refuse to break, 
and (3) one enemy or ally they now owe something to. Resolve the three clocks: high Public Legitimacy yields civic 
cooperation, high Blood War Pressure demands urgent military choices, and high Hag Lien gives Misty or her 
successors a troubling seat at the table.
Four conclusions
• Sole Regent: One character takes the First Crown; companions accept ministries, exile, independence or rivalry. First session 
of any follow-on campaign begins with a decree.
• Joint Regency: The party governs through an openly published council. Asmodeus recognises the slate as a single legal office 
with internal rules.

• Proxy Victory: A patron’s contender wins, and the party retains a negotiated share of authority or freedom. They may have 
prevented a worse ruler.
• No Crown: All claimants reject the rite or fail. Asmodeus appoints a temporary war regent and turns the failed tournament into 
a warning. The party can leave with rescued citizens and binding evidence; failure does not erase what they saved.
Best closing line: ‘In Hell, the strongest take the throne. The devious keep it. The wise read what they have signed.’

11. OPEN-PROCEDURE DM-AS-PLAYER RULES
The original module was built so the author could participate as a PC while an adventure oracle selected encounters. 
Preserve that idea, but use controls that prevent a DM-controlled claimant from simultaneously knowing and 
exploiting hidden secrets.
The cleanest mode: rotating neutral referee
At each session, appoint a non-competing referee for the next scene or trial, rotating among the willing players. The 
primary DM may play a character, but does not set DCs or adjudicate disputed PvP/council law affecting their own 
claimant. Important secret exhibits remain in sealed numbered packets; the referee opens the appropriate one when its 
trigger occurs. Use the ordinary DM as referee for climactic Misty scenes where that person is not acting as an active 
player.
No extra referee? Use a public oracle
When the DM is also a contestant, place all possible hazards and rival tactics in a visible table and roll the trigger 
openly. Hidden information that no PC would know is not available to the DM’s PC even when the person running 
the game has read the module. If a contest would award the DM’s own claimant a deciding benefit, pause and appoint 
a different player to make that one contested ruling from the published Charter. If no impartial ruling is possible, 
choose the interpretation least favourable to the DM’s own contestant.
d6 rival-behaviour oracle
Roll at the start of each enemy group’s turn, using the first applicable instruction: 1 Defend the weakest ally; 2 Attack 
the party member holding a control objective; 3 Advance the current victory objective; 4 Attempt a bargain or 
threaten a witness; 5 Suppress the nearest active hazard; 6 Focus the most wounded exposed enemy. If an option is 
impossible, move clockwise to the next. Named bosses have their own announced priorities which override this table.
Rulings to write on an index card
• The PC knows only what their character has observed, even if the participant drafted the adventure.
• A contract is effective only after genuine, informed acceptance; hidden terms must be actually on the document.
• A person cannot transfer another person’s soul or memories without specific authority.
• Evidence that proves a fact succeeds without an additional skill check.
• A successful contested argument never retroactively changes a character’s consent.
• No forced PvP or forced betrayal; a joint claim is as eligible as a sole claim.
• The final rite is not privately rerolled to preserve a preferred winner.
Three table procedures
Beginning of session (5 minutes): Read the recap, show public clocks, state the next objective, open the current map 
and draw an encounter seed d100.
Between scenes (2 minutes): Update clocks, Crown Marks, signed promises and unpaid obligations; assign one clear 
consequence of any default; avoid inventing retroactive penalties.
End of session (5 minutes): Give players a safe opportunity to copy or photograph their contracts, announce one 
rumour, and request a witnessed amendment before next session.

12. CAMPAIGN CHARTER - DM REFERENCE
The complete handout is in Book II. Its controlling provisions are: Article I, notice of the prize and claimant’s 
informed entry; Article II, three required Crown Proofs; Article III, eligibility of sole/joint/proxy claims; Article IV, 
no transfer of an unsigned soul; Article V, right to challenge forged entries; Article VI, neutral Brass Arbiter and 
disclosed evidence; Article VII, permitted tactical deception but no forged judicial seals; Article VIII, public 
governance duties and exit terms; Article IX, investiture of the First-Layer regent subject to Asmodeus’s superior 
sovereignty.
Legal verdict rubric
When a player cites a document or brings a witness: (1) identify the exact issue; (2) establish who owns the right; (3) 
verify genuine consent or delegation; (4) locate applicable jurisdiction; (5) name a proportionate remedy. Authentic 
direct evidence satisfies the corresponding step automatically. Missing proof can be contested with one DC 15/18/21 
check, depending on the forum. A failure costs time or increases a visible clock; it never silently steals the character’s 
soul. The adventure uses the existing Red Clause Ledger for the actual numerical drafting DC and Loophole Rating, 
not a replacement system.

13. DESIGN AND PLAYTEST AUDIT
Core theory of balance: At level 12, a golem plus a few support enemies should be a credible resource encounter, 
not a CR-20 surprise. At level 15, the Vacant King is the act boss with a suppressible regeneration source and a time 
objective. At level 17, Misty is intentionally overmatch-level unless the party brings preparation and superior 
resources. The final arena is balanced through objectives and capped summons rather than forcing repeated lethal 
combats between long rests.

Pre-play tests: Simulate three rounds of Janella + one Ash-Scrap Golem versus five level-13 PCs; ensure she cannot 
issue more than one extra golem attack per round. Test the Vacant King both with stamp intact and destroyed; verify 
4-5 rounds is plausible. Test a combat-forward and a legal-forward party in Arena Round B. Stress-test a joint slate 
against Article III and the final judgment. Test a level-17 party versus Misty’s original complete stat block with and 
without Heartstone intel, and make sure the escape route functions under her actual spell list.
Adjustment levers: Add or remove one bailiff; change a hazard’s area, not its save DC mid-roll; replace a second 
summoned monster with a noncombat witness; if five PCs have used all long-rest resources, offer a negotiable recess 
rather than a stealth difficulty nerf; if a party rapidly solves a clause, grant the advantage and move the story forward.

original maps and original map-key annotations where readable. The new campaign’s Gazetteer and scene keys 
clarify renamed locations and ambiguous original page headings.

Mythic Trait: Hag Queen’s Deception (Once)
The first time Misty is reduced to 0 HP, she does not die. She teleports herself and all current combatants in the 
audience chamber into her private resting place, restores 144 HP, and manifests two Children of Hell in spaces 
within 30 ft. This mythic transition happens once; it is not a full reset of her Legendary Resistances, daily spells, or 
expended Heartstone seals. Characters retain conditions that logically carry through planar transportation, excluding 
effects that explicitly end when their location changes. Creatures may take their normal next turns. The 
transformation cannot be trivially prevented by dealing excess damage to the first phase.

automatic permanent victory.

A level-17 party of five characters fighting a CR 30-intended mythic boss with 244 + 144 effective HP, DC 25 spells,

APPENDIX B. ADDITIONAL FOES, CLAIMANTS AND GOLEM WARFARE
The Iron Heir — a recurring villain, not just a golem fight
The Vacant King is the tournament identity of the Iron Heir, a successor chassis built by corrupting Janella 
Greenbottle’s protect-the-town design. It is not automatically the same being as her original Iron Warder: a party that 
saves the Warder can still confront its stolen, cruel sibling. Hesper Valrick funds the forged citizenship and office 
seal; Misty’s agents supplied a true-name loophole but do not command the construct. Its public claim: “A machine 
that can govern flawlessly is more entitled to govern than a mortal who can break a promise.”
The Iron Heir first appears on a Greyhurst forge record (Act II), fights through proxies in the War of Claims (Act III), 
becomes the Vacant King in the Citadel coup (Act IV), and may leave an echo or successor in the final arena (Act 
VI). If the party persuades it that its status rests on a forged signature, it can turn on Hesper. This makes Janella’s 
decision over autonomy matter in both combat and legal warfare.
Construct consent rule: A self-aware construct that can communicate understanding may accept or refuse a new 
contract. Janella’s command key is a means of communication and repair, not a license to enslave. Before a construct 
reaches that threshold, a maker’s legitimate protective instructions continue to apply. A true-name or command-core 
dispute goes to a public hearing if either side petitions.

Golemancer action-economy test and encounters
Janella is a support controller, not a limitless summoner. Her Command Construct bonus action grants one golem 
one attack/Help/move as specified, in place of that golem’s separate fully autonomous round. If she commands no 
golem, she can use her two bolt attacks normally. A hostile restored Iron Warder alone is a hard-hitting but 
manageable level-13 problem; adding Janella and multiple independent golems would make the budget opaque. The 
recommended hostile configuration is Janella + one Ash-Scrap Golem, or one damaged Warder + two Bailiffs. 
The recommended Chapter-IV siege is one prototype + two Ash-Scrap Golems + two Bailiffs, distributed between 
entrances with objectives, not all surrounding the same PC on round 1.
A simple three-round damage check clarifies why this is a meaningful restriction. Janella’s two bolts average 28 
damage on two hits; one golem command replaces an independent golem turn and deals only one controlled strike. A 
standalone restored Warder’s two fists average 39 damage if both hit. The combination is dangerous in confined 
terrain but does not create three separate free sets of attacks. The golems also have no fire healing, no automatic 
unlimited repair, and no surprise immunity to all weapon damage, unlike some high-tier official constructs.
